\begin{abstract}
A results table marks a winner in bold and asks the reader to believe it. We
ask how often the page prints what checking would need. From every main-track
paper in the AAMAS proceedings of 2024, 2025 and 2026 (\nAPapers{} papers) we
extract the results tables that mark a winner, identify the claim each bold
cell makes against the best value the authors did not mark, and record whether
both values carry an error bar and whether the paper states how many runs
produced them. Of \nAComparisons{} highlighted comparisons, \nAPctNeither{}
print no error bar on either side and \nAPctTestable{} can be tested from the
page. Of those that can, \nAPctFail{} are not distinguishable from zero by
Welch's $t$ at the authors' own run counts, \nRunsLeThreeFail{} where three
seeds or fewer are stated and \nRunsGeSixFail{} at six or more. The same
instrument, run unchanged on \nMlPapers{} NeurIPS and ICML papers of 2025,
finds \nMlPctNeither{} bar-less, \nMlPctTestable{} testable and
\nMlPctFail{} failing: the highlighted comparisons of AAMAS carry uncertainty
more often than those of the machine-learning mainstream, and the uncertainty
they carry supports the claim no more often. Win and success rates have a
second resolution that needs no error bar, the binomial noise of the
evaluation protocol; only \nProtoStateEpisodesPct{} of papers state how many
evaluation episodes a rate averages over, and where one does,
\nResBelowPct{} of its highlighted gaps fall below what that many episodes
can resolve. The instrument was frozen before it met this corpus: on a
reference set drawn from it, an image-based reader agrees with the parser on
the whole claim in \nGClaim{} of comparisons and on the verdict in
\nGVerdict{}, and a reading of random pages shows what the parser misses on
two-column pages. We release the pipeline, the per-comparison database and
the rendered reference set.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-agent research decides what works by comparing numbers in tables. A
method is trained under a handful of seeds, evaluated over some number of
episodes, and its mean is set in bold beside the means of its rivals. The bold
is the claim. Whether a reader can check it depends on two things the page
either prints or does not: a measure of run-to-run spread for both values, and
the number of runs behind them. Without the first, the gap between two means
is a gap between two draws; without the second, an error bar has no scale.

The community knows this. Recommendations on how many seeds to run, how to
report intervals, and how to evaluate cooperative multi-agent learning in
particular have been published and are cited
\cite{henderson2017deep,colas2018how,agarwal2021deep,gorsane2022towards,patterson2024empirical}.
What has not been measured is how often the proceedings follow them: not a
sample of papers read by hand, but every results table in a conference, with
one rule applied to each. This paper measures it for AAMAS.

We take every main-track paper in the IFAAMAS proceedings of 2024, 2025 and
2026, \nAPapers{} papers in all, and extract from each the results tables
whose bold marks a winner. In every row or column with exactly one bold cell
we take the comparison the bold makes: the marked value against the best
value the authors did not mark. For each we record whether the two values
carry error bars, what the bars are said to be, and how many runs the paper
states produced them. Where all three are present we ask whether the gap is
distinguishable from zero at that run count, by Welch's $t$ on the printed
means and deviations. The instrument is a parser and a set of rules. It was
written for a study of NeurIPS and ICML tables, validated there against an
image-based reader on reference sets drawn after it was frozen, and run here
without change, so that the two literatures can be compared under one
definition.

The findings are these. \nAPctNeither{} of the \nAComparisons{} highlighted
comparisons in AAMAS carry no error bar on either side, and \nAPctTestable{}
can be tested from the page. On the mainstream sample the same instrument
finds \nMlPctNeither{} and \nMlPctTestable{}. The difference is not that
AAMAS authors print uncertainty more often (\nAPctPapersErr{} of AAMAS
papers print any error bar, against \nMlPctPapersErr{}) but that its
highlighted comparisons are concentrated in the learning community, whose
tables carry a deviation as a matter of habit. What the deviations then show
is no better: \nAPctFail{} of the testable comparisons do not survive the
test at the stated run count, against \nMlPctFail{} in the mainstream, and
\nRunsLeThreeFail{} of those resting on three seeds or fewer. A second,
venue-specific resolution comes from the evaluation protocol itself: a win
rate over $K$ episodes has a binomial standard error whether or not anyone
prints one, and \nResBelowPct{} of the win-rate gaps whose papers state $K$
fall below what $K$ episodes can resolve. Only \nProtoStateEpisodesPct{} of
papers state $K$.

None of these numbers is exact, and the paper says how inexact. Conventions
the source papers leave unstated move the failure rate across the span of
Table~\ref{tab:span}; a reference set drawn from this corpus after the
instrument was frozen puts its agreement with an image-based reader at
\nGClaim{} on the whole claim and \nGVerdict{} on the verdict; and a reading
of \nPagesRead{} random pages shows that on two-column pages the parser lists
\nPagesReaderFoundPct{} of the single-bold groups the reader lists, and that
the ones it misses print bars less often than the ones it keeps. The
instrument is not a substitute for reading a paper. It is a way of asking one
question of \nATablesDetected{} tables at once, with an error that is
measured rather than assumed.

\section{What is measured}
\label{sec:what}

\paragraph{The unit.} A \emph{comparison} is one bold cell in a results table
set against the best non-bold cell in its group, where the group is the row or
the column within which the table marks winners. Orientation is decided per
table by which axis carries exactly one bold cell per line, with a cut into
dataset blocks where the lines agree on one; tables whose bold cells fall
several to a line, captions announcing first, second and third best marking,
and cells tied with their rival are left out (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}
counts them). Direction is read from the header's arrows and metric names,
and a bold cell that is not the extreme of its group in that direction is
discarded rather than guessed at.

\paragraph{Testability.} A comparison is \emph{testable} when both cells carry
an error bar and a run count is available: from the caption, from the table's
page, or, failing both, the smallest count stated anywhere in the paper.
Standard errors and confidence intervals need no count. An unlabelled bar is
read as a standard deviation, the reading that makes the claim easiest to
support; Section~\ref{sec:support} gives the other reading.

\paragraph{The test.} On a testable comparison we compute Welch's $t$ from the
two means, the two deviations and the run count, and call the comparison
\emph{failing} when $p \geq 0.05$ two-sided. A failure says the printed
evidence does not support the claim at the stated run count, not that the
claim is false; with three seeds the test has little power, and that is the
point. A two-sample $z$ that takes the printed deviations as exact gives a
lower rate and is reported beside it; Holm's correction within a table, where
several comparisons share one family, gives a higher one.

\paragraph{What the instrument cannot see.} It reads the text layer of the
PDF, so a table rendered as an image, uncertainty drawn in a figure, and
shading that encodes significance are invisible to it and counted as absent.
It cannot tell whether two methods share seeds, so the test treats them as
independent; Table~\ref{tab:span} carries a paired reading. It does not re-run
anything: everything here is a statement about the page.

\paragraph{Frozen.} The parser and every rule above were fixed and tagged
before this corpus was downloaded. Whatever the reference set of
Section~\ref{sec:instrument} exposed is reported there and left in place, so
that the precision figures describe the instrument that produced the numbers.

\section{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

The IFAAMAS proceedings are open. From their tables of contents we take every
paper listed under the main track (``Full Papers'' in 2024, ``Research Paper
Track'' in 2025 and 2026) and leave out extended abstracts, demonstrations,
the doctoral consortium, JAAMAS and Blue Sky papers: \nAPapersTwentyFour{},
\nAPapersTwentyFive{} and \nAPapersTwentySix{} papers, \nAPapers{} in all.
Every one is an eight-page ACM two-column document with an unlimited
reference list and no appendix in the proceedings PDF, which matters for what
follows: whatever a reader can check has to be on those eight pages.

The comparison sample is the \nMlPapers{} NeurIPS and ICML papers of 2025,
single-column with appendices, extracted by the same code and reported
beside AAMAS throughout.

To read the AAMAS corpus by community, a language model assigned each paper
one of eight labels from its title and first page: learning; game theory and
social choice; LLM-based agents; simulation; planning and search; reasoning,
norms and verification; robotics and human--agent interaction; other. The
prompt, the model and every answer are released, and Table~\ref{tab:community}
gives the split. The label is a reading of a first page, not the area the
authors submitted to.

\section{What the printed evidence supports}
\label{sec:support}

Of the \nANTestable{} testable comparisons, from \nAPapersTestable{} papers,
\nAPctFail{} are not distinguishable from zero by Welch's $t$ at the stated
run count (\nAPctFailCi{} over papers), and \nAPctFailHolm{} after Holm's
correction within the table. The median relative gap among the failing
comparisons is \nAGapFail{}, among the surviving ones \nAGapPass{}. A
two-sample $z$ on the same comparisons, each printed deviation taken as
exact, fails \nSpanZTwoSidedFail{}. On the mainstream sample the
corresponding rates are \nMlPctFail{} and \nMlPctFailHolm{}: the AAMAS
interval covers both, and the claim that AAMAS does better once it prints a
deviation is not one the data support.

\paragraph{Seeds.} The failure rate follows the run count
(Figure~\ref{fig:census}b). At three seeds or fewer (\nRunsLeThreeN{}
comparisons) \nRunsLeThreeFail{} fail; at four or five (\nRunsFourToFiveN{})
\nRunsFourToFiveFail{}; at six or more (\nRunsGeSixN{}) \nRunsGeSixFail{}.
Part of this is the arithmetic of the $t$ distribution: at $n=3$ a gap must
exceed about two and a half pooled deviations to reach $p<0.05$, and few of
the gaps here do. It is also the pattern the seed-count literature predicted
\cite{colas2018how,agarwal2021deep} and the reason
\citet{gorsane2022towards} asked for ten. A power reading says the same
thing from the other side: for \nPowerUnderpowered{} of the \nPowerN{}
comparisons with a deviation and a count, the reported gap is smaller than
the minimum detectable difference at 80\% power for that design, whichever
way the test came out.

\paragraph{Environments.} The captions name where \nEnvNamedTestablePct{}
of the testable comparisons come from. The StarCraft family (SMAC and
SMACv2) holds \nEnvStarcraftTestable{} testable comparisons from
\nEnvStarcraftPapers{} papers and \nEnvStarcraftFail{} of them fail; the
MuJoCo, D4RL and control tables, which multi-agent papers share with
single-agent RL, hold \nEnvMujocoTestable{} from \nEnvMujocoPapers{} papers
at \nEnvMujocoFail{}; the Atari and game tables \nEnvAtariTestable{} at
\nEnvAtariFail{}. These are a handful of papers each and the rates are
theirs, not the environments'; what they share is win rates and returns
averaged over few seeds with deviations of the same order as the gaps.

a separate claim; it is reported, not preferred.

\section{The resolution of the evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

A win rate or a success rate is a proportion, and a proportion measured over
$K$ episodes has a standard error of $\sqrt{p(1-p)/K}$ whether or not anyone
prints it. Two rates measured over $K$ episodes each cannot be told apart
when their gap is below $1.96\sqrt{p_1(1-p_1)/K + p_2(1-p_2)/K}$, and no
bold face changes that. This resolution is a property of the protocol, and
it is the one quantity in this paper that can be computed for a bar-less
comparison from what the page prints, provided the page prints $K$.

\paragraph{What the papers state.} We searched every paper's text for a
statement of the number of evaluation episodes, rollouts or test games a
reported rate averages over, and for a statement of the number of seeds.
\nProtoStateEpisodes{} of the \nProtoPapersScanned{} papers
(\nProtoStateEpisodesPct{}) state the former in a form the patterns match
and \nProtoStateSeeds{} (\nProtoStateSeedsPct{}) the latter; the commonest
stated episode count is \nProtoModeEpisodes{}, in \nProtoModeEpisodesN{}
papers. The patterns and every matched sentence are released, and both
counts are lower bounds on what a reader could find, since a protocol
described in words the patterns do not match is missed.

\paragraph{Gaps below resolution.} \nResRateLike{} highlighted comparisons
are of rates, by the words in their caption or header (win, success, solved)
and their range; \nResWithK{} of them come from papers that state $K$, and
those from only \nResPapers{} papers. Taking $K$ episodes per seed times the
stated seeds, or one seed where none is stated (\nResSeedAssumedOne{} of
these comparisons, a reading that favours the claim), \nResBelowPct{} of the
gaps fall below the resolution of the protocol that produced them
(\nResBelowCi{} over comparisons, \nResBelowPaperCi{} over papers). Among the
\nResBarlessN{} that print no error bar, \nResBarlessBelow{} are below it;
among the \nResBarredN{} that do, \nResBarredBelow{}. Seven papers are too
few to carry a rate for the venue, and we do not offer one; the finding is
that the quantity can be computed at all, that where it can it fails about
half the claims it meets, and that for nine papers in ten it cannot be
computed because the page does not say how many episodes were played.

\section{How far to trust the instrument here}
\label{sec:instrument}

The instrument was built and validated on single-column NeurIPS and ICML
PDFs. AAMAS papers are two-column ACM documents with narrower tables, tables
that span both columns, and captions that sit beside another column's text.
The precision figures from the earlier corpus do not transfer; they have to
be measured again, and so does recall.

\paragraph{Reference set.} After the parser was frozen we drew \nGDrawnN{}
comparisons from the AAMAS corpus at random, \nGTestableN{} testable and
\nGNoBarN{} bar-less after the exclusion below, rendered each table at twice
its printed size with the cell under audit boxed in red, and had an
image-based reader (a vision-language model, named in the AI use statement)
record, blind to the parser's output, whether the region is a results table,
whether the boxed cell is bold and alone in its group, the orientation and
direction, both means and bars, the kind of bar, and the run count from the
paper's seed and run sentences supplied in full. The reader agrees with the
parser on the whole claim---bold cell, rival and all four values---in
\nGClaimFrac{} fully readable items (\nGClaim{}; \nGClaimPaperCi{}
resampling papers), on the bold cell in \nGBoldCellFrac{}, the winner's mean
in \nGWinnerMeanFrac{}, its bar in \nGWinnerDispFrac{}, the runner-up in
\nGRunnerMeanFrac{}, its bar in \nGRunnerDispFrac{}, and the run count in
\nGNRunsFrac{}. On the verdict, which is what the headline rests on, the two
agree on \nGVerdictFrac{} of the comparisons both can test (\nGVerdict{});
from the reader's own values the failure rate on those items is
\nGFailReader{} against \nGFailPipe{} from the parser's. The reader could
not test \nGUntestableN{} of the parser's testable items, mostly for want of
a run count it would accept. Of the \nGNoBarN{} comparisons the parser found
bar-less, the reader finds no bar for either cell anywhere in the table in
\nGNoBarAbsentFrac{} (\nGNoBarAbsent{}).

\paragraph{What the reference set exposed.} \nGUnboxedN{} of the
\nGDrawnN{} items could not be rendered with a box: the parser holds two
records for one table on the page, a body attached to the neighbouring
column's text as its caption or a table split in two, and the kit looked up
the cell in the wrong record. The reader's answers on those items are void
and they are excluded above. The artefact is not confined to the kit:
\nADupKeys{} of the \nATableKeys{} table records in the corpus are such
duplicates, holding \nADupComparisons{} comparisons (\nADupPct{} of the
corpus), and it is left in place, as the frozen rule requires. Where the
reader and parser disagree on a claim, the reasons are the ones the earlier
corpus showed---a table that bolds every entry of the authors' row, a tie
between two bold cells the parser resolved and the reader did not, a rival
taken from a reference column---and are listed in the supplement.

These are agreements between two extractors of different technology, one on
the text layer and one on the rendered image, not precision against a human
reference, and the box anchors the reader on the parser's choice of winner
though not on its values.

\paragraph{Random pages.} Precision does not bound recall. We drew one page
holding a table caption at random from each of \nPagesPapers{} papers
outside the reference set and had the reader list, with nothing marked, every
results table on the page, every row or column with exactly one bold cell,
and for each the bars and run count. \nPagesTables{} of the tables it lists
are results tables and \nPagesTablesWithBar{} of those print any bar. The
reader finds \nPagesReaderGroups{} single-bold groups, of which
\nPagesReaderNeither{} are bar-less and \nPagesReaderTestable{} testable;
the parser lists \nPagesPipeGroups{} on the same pages, \nPagesPipeNeither{}
bar-less and \nPagesPipeTestable{} testable. The parser lists
\nPagesReaderFoundPct{} of the reader's groups (\nPagesReaderFound{}), less
than half of what it managed on single-column pages, and the reader lists
\nPagesPipeFoundPct{} of the parser's. On most pages where the parser misses
the reader's groups it has detected the table and produced no comparison
from it, by its own rules: bold that is not the extreme of its line, lines
with more than one bold cell, a block cut the columns do not agree on. The
groups it misses print bars less often than the groups it keeps
(\nPagesUnmatchedNeither{} bar-less against \nPagesMatchedNeither{}), so
the census headline, taken over what the parser reads, understates the
bar-less share: over every group the reader finds, the share is
\nPagesCensusRatio{} times the parser's on the same pages, which would put
the headline at \nANeitherCensusScaled{}. The testable share moves the other
way. Every number in Sections~\ref{sec:print} and~\ref{sec:support} is
therefore a statement about the \nAComparisons{} comparisons the parser
reads, with a precision measured on the reference set and a coverage
measured here.

\paragraph{What the funnel discards.} Of \nATablesDetected{} tables
detected, \nATablesYielding{} yield at least one comparison. Among
\nAGroupsCandidate{} candidate groups, \nAGroupsUsablePct{} have exactly one
bold cell and a rival; \nABoldNotExtremalPct{} of single-bold groups have a
bold cell that is not the extreme of its group in the read direction and are
dropped, and \nABoldTied{} groups tie with their rival.
\nAEmptyTablesCaptionUncertainty{} of the \nAEmptyTables{} tables without a
cell-level bar have a caption that mentions uncertainty, which the parser
cannot use.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The case for seeds and intervals in reinforcement learning was made by
\citet{henderson2017deep}, \citet{islam2017reproducibility},
\citet{colas2018how,colas2019hitchhiker}, \citet{jordan2020evaluating},
\citet{chan2020measuring} and \citet{agarwal2021deep}, and for cooperative
multi-agent learning by \citet{gorsane2022towards}, who read the evaluation
sections of MARL papers by hand and found most reporting a handful of seeds
without intervals. Benchmarks and libraries followed
\cite{papoudakis2021benchmarking,ellis2023smacv2,bettini2024benchmarl}, and
\citet{patterson2024empirical} set out what an empirical design in RL should
contain. The recommendations are settled; this paper measures their uptake in
a whole proceedings rather than a sample, and asks the further question of
whether the evidence that is printed supports the claim it sits beside.

Studies of reporting practice in machine learning at large
\cite{dodge2019show,bouthillier2021accounting,christodoulou2025false,hutchinson2022evaluation}
read papers by hand in the hundreds; the instrument here reads tables by the
thousand and pays for it in a measured error. Re-running literatures
\cite{henderson2017deep,musgrave2020metric,dacrema2019really} asks whether
gains hold up; we ask only whether the page lets a reader tell. The
evaluation-overfitting and protocol literature
\cite{whiteson2011protecting,machado2018revisiting,balduzzi2018reevaluating}
concerns what is measured; this paper concerns what is printed about it. The
instrument itself, its bugs and its validation on the machine-learning
mainstream are described in a concurrent submission; this paper is its first
use on another field's proceedings, its first measurement on a two-column
format, and the first measurement of protocol resolution.

\section{What to print}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three lines would make most of these tables checkable. The number of seeds,
in the caption of every table that averages over them. A deviation or a
standard error beside every mean, labelled as one or the other. The number of
evaluation episodes behind every rate. The learning papers at AAMAS do the
second more often than the mainstream, and the test then shows how thin the
evidence is at three or five seeds; the third is stated by fewer than one
paper in ten, though it is the one quantity that resolves a win rate with no
error bar at all. A reader who finds a bold cell in these proceedings can
check it on the page less than one time in three, and when the check can be
made, it fails about as often as it passes.

\section{Contents of the supplementary archive}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{study/}: the instrument at its frozen commit (parser, comparison
  rules, tests, reference-set tooling), unchanged from the tag it was frozen at.
\item \texttt{aamas/}: the AAMAS-specific scripts: the proceedings index, the
  analysis driver, the protocol scan, the community classifier, the macro
  generator, the figure.
\item \texttt{data/extracted\_aamas.jsonl}: every table cell the parser read
  from the \nAPapers{} papers, with position, bold flag, mean and bar.
\item \texttt{out/aamas/}: every run record a number in the paper comes from,
  including the per-comparison database (\texttt{analysis.json}), the
  community labels, the protocol sentences, and the reference-set scores.
\item \texttt{audits/aamas\_gold/}: the \nGTestableN{} testable and
  \nGNoBarN{} bar-less reference items, rendered, with the reader's labels.
\item \texttt{audits/aamas\_gold\_gemini/}: the second reader's labels on the
  same reference items.
\item \texttt{audits/aamas\_pages/}: the \nPagesRead{} random pages of the
  pilot with the first reader's end-to-end labels.
\item \texttt{audits/aamas\_census/}: the \nCzPagesDrawn{} caption pages of
  the census with the second reader's labels (\nCzPagesRead{} read).
\item \texttt{audits/aamas\_testable\_pages/}: the \nTpPages{} pages holding
  parser-testable comparisons, read a second time for the test--retest check.
\item \texttt{audits/aamas\_census\_effort/}, \texttt{aamas\_census\_zero/},
  \texttt{aamas\_noncaption/}: the reasoning-effort check, the re-read of
  the pages on which the census listed nothing, and the caption-less pages
  (Section~\ref{app:census}).
\item \texttt{audits/gold\_f/}: the concurrent study's \nScPages{} random
  mainstream pages with both readers' labels; \texttt{out/aamas/protocol\_reader2.json}:
  the extended protocol reading of \nPtwoPapers{} papers.
\item \texttt{run\_all\_aamas.sh}: regenerates every number and the macros.
\end{itemize}

\section{The census protocol}
\label{app:census}
Every page on which the parser detected a table caption (\nCzPagesDrawn{}
pages from \nCzPapersDrawn{} papers) was rendered at twice its printed size
and sent, with the paper's run-count sentences, to the second reader (Gemini
3.1 Pro, temperature 0) under the instructions below, emitted verbatim from
\texttt{study/read\_pages\_api.py} by the build. The protocol was fixed
before the reading began (\texttt{git tag census-protocol-v1}). Pages whose
answer was not valid JSON after three attempts were retried in a separate
run. Fifteen pages, among the densest in the corpus, timed out at the relay
on every attempt at the model's default reasoning setting (one of them
carries a hundred single-bold groups over three tables); they were read
with the reasoning effort set to \texttt{low}, everything else identical.
To see what that setting changes, \nRePages{} pages the census had read at
the default setting, drawn at random among those listing a group, were read
again at \texttt{low}: the second read matches \nReMatchedCi{} of the first
read's \nReGroupsFirst{} groups and lists \nReGroupsSecond{}; the bar-less
verdict agrees on \nReNeitherAgreePct{} of the matched groups, testability on
\nReTestableAgreePct{}, and the Welch verdict on \nReFailAgree{} of the
\nReBothTested{} both could test; bar-less shares \nReNeitherFirst{} against
\nReNeitherSecond{}, failure rates \nReFailFirst{} against \nReFailSecond{}
(\texttt{out/aamas/reader\_effort.json}). \nCzPagesRead{} of the
\nCzPagesDrawn{} pages were read. The reading is
scored by \texttt{study/pages\_score.py}, the same script that scores the
pilot, matching each reader group to at most one parser comparison on the
winner's printed value.

The \nTpPages{} pages holding parser-testable comparisons had been read by
the same model in an earlier run under the same instructions; on the
\nRtPages{} of them the census read, the second
read matches \nRtMatchedCi{} of the first read's \nRtGroupsFirst{} groups
(\nRtMatchedOfSecondPct{} of the second read's \nRtGroupsSecond{}), the
bar-less verdict agrees on \nRtNeitherAgree{} of the \nRtMatched{} matched
groups (\nRtNeitherAgreePct{}), testability on \nRtTestableAgreePct{}, and
the Welch verdict on \nRtFailAgree{} of the \nRtBothTested{} groups both
reads could test; the failure rates of the two reads are \nRtFailFirst{}
and \nRtFailSecond{} (\texttt{aamas/reader\_retest.py}).

Every reader group whose bold cell is not the better value in the
stated direction, or ties its rival, is dropped before scoring
(\texttt{--extremal-only} in \texttt{study/pages\_score.py}, and always
in \texttt{aamas/reader\_retest.py}), as the parser's rules drop such
groups; the concurrent study scores its readers without that rule.
On the \nCcrPages{} random pilot pages both readers read, the first lists
\nCcrFirstGroups{} groups and the second \nCcrSecondGroups{}, \nCcrMatched{}
of which match (\nCcrMatchedPct{} of the first's).
\nCcrUnmatchedOnEmptyPages{} of the \nCcrFirstUnmatched{} unmatched
first-reader groups sit on the \nCcrPagesSecondEmpty{} pages the census
reader returned with no group; four of those tables it read as having
several bold cells per line or bold on the authors' row (its notes are in
the labels file), and one is a true dropout that a second read lists. The
parser keeps a table whose bold row is extremal on each line and cuts
columns into blocks where lines agree on a break; the census protocol
has neither rule, so part of that disagreement is the unit. Over the
whole census, \nEcPages{} of the \nEcParserPages{} pages on which the
parser reads a comparison came back with no group: \nEcNonePages{} that
the reader called results tables with no single winner per line
(\nEcNoneComparisons{} parser comparisons, \nEcNoneNeither{} bar-less,
\nEcNoneTestable{} testable) and \nEcNotablePages{} it called no results
table (\nEcNotableComparisons{} comparisons). No human has looked at
either set. On the \nCcrSharedPages{} pages both list groups the second reader
matches \nCcrSharedMatchedPct{} of the first's, the bar-less shares are
\nCcrSharedFirstNeither{} and \nCcrSharedSecondNeither{}, the testable
shares \nCcrSharedFirstTestable{} and \nCcrSharedSecondTestable{}, and
the matched groups agree on the bar-less verdict in
\nCcrSharedNeitherAgreePct{} and on the Welch verdict in
\nCcrSharedFailAgree{} of \nCcrSharedBothTested{}. The same decomposition
on the \nMsPages{} mainstream pages: \nMsCrossPagesSecondEmpty{} pages
returned empty by the census reader, and on the \nMsCrossSharedPages{}
pages both list, \nMsCrossSharedMatchedPct{} matched with bar-less shares
\nMsCrossSharedFirstNeither{} and \nMsCrossSharedSecondNeither{}
(\texttt{out/aamas/reader\_cross\_pilot.json}, \texttt{reader\_cross\_ml.json}).

\section{The evaluation-protocol reader}
\label{app:protocol-reader}
Model: Gemini 3.1 Pro (Google), temperature 0, one call per paper with the
first 60,000 characters of the paper's text. The first reading (six
fields, every paper highlighting a rate under an earlier, looser rate
pattern; \texttt{out/aamas/protocol\_reader.json}) used the prompt below.
The reading the paper's Section~6 rests on, written by
\texttt{aamas/protocol\_reader2.py} to
\texttt{out/aamas/protocol\_reader2.json},
covers every learning-labelled paper, every paper highlighting a rate and
every paper with a testable comparison, \nPtwoPapers{} in all, and adds
whether the evaluation episodes are sampled or a fixed instance set, the
environments, single- or multi-agent, the codebase, whether the paper
says it follows a named benchmark's standard setup without stating $K$,
whether the compared methods share evaluation episodes, and what the
printed deviation is over. Its prompt follows the first. No human read
any of the papers; the check is between the model and the regular
expressions of Section~\ref{app:patterns}, and between two prompts to
the same model.

\section{Evaluation-protocol patterns}
\label{app:patterns}
The sentences that state an evaluation episode count or a seed count were
found with the regular expressions in \texttt{aamas/protocol.py}; every match
is in \texttt{out/aamas/protocol.json} with its sentence. A count is the
smallest stated in the paper; a paper stating none is counted as not stating
one, which under-counts protocols described in words the patterns miss.

\section{The protocol-resolution comparisons by paper}
\label{app:protocol-papers}
Section~6 of the paper works on the corpus with the \nSixdedupRemoved{}
exact-copy comparisons removed (\nSixdedupN{} remain). A rate comparison
passes five tests (\texttt{aamas/make\_all\_aamas.py}, \texttt{build\_rate\_set}):
its table's caption or header names a win, winning, success, solved or
completion rate; the record is anchored on a caption beginning ``Table'';
the bold marks the higher value; the group's own row label and column
header, read from the PDF text layer around the parser's coordinates by
\texttt{aamas/rate\_labels.py} (\texttt{out/aamas/rate\_group\_labels.json}),
name no other metric; and its values sit on the table's scale, percent
where the text says so or no value in the table is a proportion. Of
\nRfTableNamesARate{} comparisons in tables naming a rate,
\nRfNotAnchoredOnACaption{} body-text windows, \nRfLowerIsBetter{}
lower-is-better columns, \nRfOutOfRange{} out-of-range values,
\nRfGroupLabelNamesAnotherMetric{} groups labelled with another metric
and \nRfOffTheTableScale{} off-scale values are removed, leaving
\nRfKept{} from \nRfKeptPapers{} papers. An earlier, table-level filter
admitted time, cost and return columns of tables whose caption named a
success rate; a reviewer of the fourth draft caught it, and no human has
checked the survivors. The first table is the model-read set the paper
reports (the fixed-instance rule, $n = K$, holds in every seed reading);
the second imputes PyMARL's default of 32 evaluation episodes to
SMAC-family papers that state none and name no other codebase; the third
is the pattern-read set. Where a paper cross-validates over a fixed
instance set, the folds are disjoint and $n$ is $K$ times the folds
(one paper, \nPtwoResTypedCvFolds{} comparisons). A rate over $n$ trials
is a multiple of $1/n$, and the tables' last column reports whether each
paper's printed values sit on that lattice within their own rounding:
\nPtwoResTypedLatticeConsistent{} of the \nPtwoResTypedN{} model-read
comparisons do (\nPtwoResTypedLatticeConsistentPapers{} papers), and
\nPtwoResInheritedLatticeConsistent{} of the \nPtwoResInheritedN{}
imputed ones. An off-lattice value means a mean over runs the page does
not count, or a misread $K$.
Of the \nPtwoPapers{} papers the extended reader covers, a few read under
the earlier rate pattern are kept in the record and left out of every
count.

\section{Who cites the methodology}
\label{app:cites}
Among the \nCcDetectedIn{} papers with a highlighted comparison,
\nCcCitingPapers{} cite at least one of the evaluation-methodology papers
named in the paper's related work (by title in the text), and they report
differently: \nCcCitingPm{} print a $\pm$ cell against \nCcNotCitingPm{}
of the rest, \nCcCitingRuns{} state a run count against
\nCcNotCitingRuns{}, and \nCcCitingK{} state an evaluation episode count
the patterns match against \nCcNotCitingK{}; their comparisons are
\nCcCitingNeither{} bar-less against \nCcNotCitingNeither{}, and the
\nCcCitingNTestable{} testable ones fail at \nCcCitingFail{} against
\nCcNotCitingFail{}. So few papers cannot say whether the citation causes
the habit or the habit the citation; they can say that the
recommendations travel with the citation and that the test result does
not.

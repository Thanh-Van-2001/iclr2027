\title{The convention on the page: an audit of \nOListedSrep{} metaheuristics papers in \emph{Scientific Reports} against their own reporting rule}

\begin{abstract}
Metaheuristics has a reporting convention: independent runs, a mean and standard deviation per function, the best in bold, a non-parametric test. We ask how much of it reaches the page in the 2024--2025 metaheuristics output of one general megajournal, \emph{Scientific Reports} (\nOListedSrep{} papers listed by OpenAlex), with released instruments under a same-day analysis plan: a model classifier of kind (\nKcNewOptimizer{} papers labelled new-optimizer, \nKcApplication{} application; a second labeller files \nKafDirSecondNewOptimizer{} as new-optimizer), a text scan and a model reading of the full text, and an image census of the table pages of \nCzPapersDrawn{} random papers. By text scan \nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} of the labelled new-optimizer papers state a run count and \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} name a test, against \nSkApplicationPctRunsStated{} and \nSkApplicationPctTestNamed{} of the applications. In the census, \nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} \nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeitherCi{} of the bold means in new-optimizer papers print no deviation, and where one is printed it sits in a separate row or column in three cases of four. Of the \nCrcMeansTested{} bold means whose printed summaries can be tested, \nCrcMeansFail{} \nCrcMeansFailCi{} are not separable from their rival by Welch's $t$ at the stated run count, a per-cell check the guides do not prescribe: three multi-objective studies hold \nCrcPpTopThreeShareMeans{} of the tested cells, and of the \nVcWelchFailLocated{} failures that sit beside a printed verdict, \nVcWelchFailPrintedSignificant{} are called significant by the paper's own rank test. Whether a per-cell verdict is printed at all is the field on which two full-text readers disagree most, one way (\nTaMarkersNewOptimizerFirstPct{} against \nTaMarkersNewOptimizerSecondPct{} of the double-read new-optimizer papers). The image reader's pairing of means with deviations is checked against a second vendor's model on two hundred released kit items, not yet against a person.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A results table in a metaheuristics paper is a promise with a shape. Each row is a benchmark function, each column an optimizer; the cell holds the mean over some number of independent runs, with the standard deviation beside it or on the line below; the best mean in the row is bold; a Wilcoxon signed-rank or Friedman test, somewhere later, says whether the bold is more than noise. The shape is not an accident. It was argued into the field's habits by García et al.\ \cite{garcia2009study,garcia2010advanced} and Derrac et al.\ \cite{derrac2011practical}, restated in every methodological guide since \cite{carrasco2020recent,latorre2021prescription,osaba2021tutorial,bartz2020benchmarking,beiranvand2017best,kendall2016good}, and enforced, to a degree, by the competitions and platforms that fix the run count, the budget and the summary statistics: the CEC special sessions and, for BBOB, COCO \cite{hansen2021coco}.

What the guides do not say is how often the shape survives contact with a page. A bold cell is only checkable if the reader can see both means, both deviations and the run count; a test is only a test if the paper says which one, on what, and paired or not. This paper measures those things on the page, with instruments that are fixed before the corpus is downloaded and released with it, so that the measurement is reproducible and its errors are stated rather than guessed at.

The measurement is deliberately narrow. It does not re-run any optimizer, does not ask whether a proposed algorithm is new (a question others have taken up \cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics,aranha2022metaphor,camacho2022exposing,tzanetos2021nature,piotrowski2018some}), and does not judge whether a benchmark suite is the right one \cite{kudela2022critical}. It asks only: given the numbers the authors chose to print, does the bold cell stand at the run count the authors state, and does the paper's own test say the same thing? The bold cell is a typographic marker of the best mean on one function; the field's statistical claim is usually an aggregate over functions, and the per-cell test here measures what the printed evidence resolves for the marker, not whether the aggregate claim holds. Where the printed numbers cannot answer, that is the finding.

The paper makes three contributions. First, a census of reporting practice over the \nOParsed{} metaheuristics papers that one open-access megajournal, \emph{Scientific Reports}, published in 2024--2025, by instruments whose precision and coverage are measured rather than assumed: how often a bold cell has a deviation and a run count on the page, how often it survives Welch's $t$ on the printed numbers, and how those rates split between papers that propose an optimizer, papers that apply one, and papers that compare several. Second, a reading of the field's own statistical convention beside the printed evidence: which tests are named, at what run count, and whether papers that cite the methodology literature report differently. Third, the released instruments, protocols and run records, so that every number here can be regenerated and the corpus rule changed by anyone who disagrees with it.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Methodology guides} The statistical convention of the field is unusually well documented. García et al.\ \cite{garcia2009study} argued from the CEC 2005 special session that parametric tests are inappropriate for optimizer comparisons and recommended the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for pairs and the Friedman test with post-hoc procedures for groups; Derrac et al.\ \cite{derrac2011practical} turned that into a tutorial in the first issue of this journal, and Carrasco et al.\ \cite{carrasco2020recent} surveyed a decade of its uptake, finding that non-parametric tests had become common while their assumptions were rarely checked. LaTorre et al.\ \cite{latorre2021prescription} and Osaba et al.\ \cite{osaba2021tutorial} give prescriptive guidelines covering the run count, the benchmark suite, the tuning of competitors and the test; Bartz-Beielstein et al.\ \cite{bartz2020benchmarking}, Beiranvand et al.\ \cite{beiranvand2017best}, Kendall et al.\ \cite{kendall2016good} and, earlier, Hooker \cite{hooker1995testing} and Eiben and Jelasity \cite{eiben2002critical} make the same case from the benchmarking and operational-research sides. Our study reads the literature that those guides address and reports, paper by paper, what of the guidance reached print.

\paragraph{Critiques of the field} A second literature asks whether new metaheuristics are new. Sörensen \cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics} called the metaphor-driven proposals of the 2000s a step backwards; Aranha et al.\ \cite{aranha2022metaphor} issued a call for action signed by editors of several journals; Camacho-Villalón et al.\ \cite{camacho2022exposing} showed six widely cited algorithms to be re-descriptions of older ones; Molina et al.\ \cite{molina2020comprehensive}, Tzanetos and Dounias \cite{tzanetos2021nature}, Velasco et al.\ \cite{velasco2024literature} and Campelo and Aranha \cite{campelo2023lessons} catalogue and taxonomise the population. Kudela \cite{kudela2022critical} showed that a common benchmark hazard, functions whose optimum lies at the origin, lets centre-biased algorithms appear to win. Our question is orthogonal to novelty and to benchmark choice: it concerns whether the evidence printed for a claim, whatever the algorithm and the suite, can be checked.

\paragraph{Audits of reporting} Statcheck \cite{nuijten2016prevalence} recomputed $p$-values from printed test statistics in psychology and found inconsistencies in half of the papers; AxCell \cite{kardas2020axcell} extracted results tables from machine-learning papers into leaderboards. The instruments here belong to that family, read the tables rather than the prose, and were first applied to two machine-learning and multi-agent venues in companion audits that are under review; this is their third application and the first to a field with an explicit convention to audit against. The parser is unchanged, and Section~\ref{sec:parser} says what that costs on this format; what is new is the corpus, the reader census as the quoted instrument, the reading of the field's own tests, and the classification by kind of paper.

\section{What is measured}
\label{sec:what}

\paragraph{The unit} A \emph{comparison} is one bold cell in a results table set against the best non-bold cell in its group, the group being the row or the column within which the table marks its winners. The parser decides the orientation per table by which axis carries exactly one bold cell per line, and reads the direction (lower is better for an error, higher for a hit rate) from the header. Tables where bold marks several cells per line, where the caption announces first, second and third best, and cells tied with their rival, are left out and counted.

\paragraph{Testability} A comparison is \emph{testable} when both cells carry a dispersion and a run count is available: from the caption, from the page, or, failing both, the smallest count stated anywhere in the paper. On the machine-learning tables the parser was frozen on, the smallest count is the reading least favourable to separating the cells, which raises the failure rate; on this corpus it is sometimes not a run count at all (Section~\ref{sec:parser}). An unlabelled dispersion is read as a standard deviation, the reading that makes the claim easiest to support; a bracketed pair is read as an interval of the mean and tested in the normal limit without a run count (on this corpus a bracketed pair under a mean is often the best and worst run, which is not an interval of the mean; the reader files \nCrcFormBracket{} results tables that way and the parser's version affects a handful of cells in Table~\ref{tab:span}).

\paragraph{The test} On a testable comparison we compute Welch's $t$ \cite{welch1947generalization} from the two means, the two deviations and the run count, with the same count on both arms since the paper states one count for all its optimizers (where the arms in fact differed, the statistic and its degrees of freedom would too), and call the comparison \emph{failing} when $p \geq 0.05$ two-sided. A failure says that the printed summaries do not separate the bold cell from its rival at the stated run count by a $t$-type test, with the same count on both arms; it does not say that the claim is false, it does not say what the authors' own test would find on the per-run values or across functions, and it says nothing about a difference the authors never asserted. Many tables bold the best mean by convention; for those, a failure measures how thin the printed evidence is. Holm's correction \cite{holm1979simple} within a table is reported beside the raw rate; it controls the family-wise error over the table's bold-cell claims, the family this audit tests, which is smaller than the family of all pairwise comparisons the authors' own correction would face, so it understates that correction.

\paragraph{The format hazard, and the two instruments} Metaheuristics tables often print the mean and the standard deviation as two rows or two columns rather than as $a \pm b$ in one cell; the parser, frozen before this corpus was chosen, reads such a mean as bar-less. This was stated in the pre-registered plan, and the image-based reader census of Section~\ref{sec:census} is the instrument this paper quotes; its protocol gains one value of one field for the separate-row layout. The parser and its rules have not been changed since the companion audits; this study adds readings on its output, and \ref{app:parser} says what those readings found.

\section{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

The plan fixed, before any download, a corpus of fully open-access CC-BY journals with a metaheuristics output, selected by an API query so that the corpus is reproducible: every article of 2024 or 2025 whose title or abstract matches a phrase list (metaheuristic, evolutionary algorithm, genetic algorithm, particle swarm, differential evolution, swarm intelligence, ant colony, simulated annealing, nature-inspired, bio-inspired optimization, grey wolf, whale optimization, memetic, NSGA) in OpenAlex.\footnote{\url{https://openalex.org}; the query and the list are in the released code.} Hybrid journals, this one included, were excluded because their open-access subset is a selected sample. The query listed \nOListed{} papers across \nOJournalsListed{} journals. Three publishers refused automated download of their PDFs. A released record of throttled requests, five random listed papers per journal at five-second intervals, gives the answers: the four MDPI journals returned no PDF to any of \nFaMdpiAttempts{} requests (status 403 or no answer; \nFaMdpiListed{} listed papers), \emph{PeerJ Computer Science} 403 behind a bot screen to all \nFaPeerjComputerScienceAttempts{}, and \emph{Complex \& Intelligent Systems} status 200 with an HTML challenge page in place of the file in \nFaComplexAndIntelligentSystemsHtmlOnly{} of \nFaComplexAndIntelligentSystemsAttempts{}; \emph{Scientific Reports} returned the PDF for \nFaScientificReportsPdfOk{} of \nFaScientificReportsAttempts{}. Europe PMC mirrors two of the journals; its search API returned the identifiers, its full-text endpoint serves the article XML, and its PDF endpoint and NCBI's answered a bot challenge, so the PDF the frozen parser reads was not obtainable that way either (the attempts are in the same record). The corpus is therefore the \emph{Scientific Reports} subset: \nOListedSrep{} papers (\nOListedSrepTwentyFour{} of 2024 and \nOListedSrepTwentyFive{} of 2025), \nOFetched{} fetched and \nOParsed{} parsed. This is a deviation from the plan: the corpus is one megajournal's metaheuristics output rather than seven journals', and no number in this paper describes the venues in which this journal's readers publish.

\paragraph{Two literatures} A language model read the title and abstract of each listed paper and assigned one kind: \emph{new optimizer} (a new, modified or hybrid metaheuristic evaluated against others), \emph{comparison} (existing optimizers benchmarked, none proposed), \emph{application} (one or more metaheuristics applied to a domain problem, the application being the contribution), \emph{survey or theory}, or \emph{other}; the prompt asked also whether the optimizer is metaphor-named and whether the abstract mentions benchmark functions. The prompt, reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}, sees the title and abstract only and names no reporting feature; its definition of a new optimizer, one evaluated against others, does select the papers that benchmark, which is where the convention applies, and the rates below, computed from the full text the classifier never saw, measure how far those papers follow it. By this reading the corpus is \nKcApplication{} application papers, \nKcNewOptimizer{} new-optimizer papers, \nKcComparison{} comparisons, \nKcSurveyTheory{} surveys and \nKcOther{} others. The ratio is a property of matching the phrase list against a general-science journal's abstracts, not of the field, and the two kinds report differently enough that every rate below is given by kind. A second vendor's model labelled a random \nKaPapers{} papers under the same prompt and agreed with the first on \nKaAgree{} (\nKaPctAgree{}, Wilson \nKaCiAgree{}; Cohen's $\kappa$ \nKaKappa{}); the disagreements run one way, the second labeller filing \nKaDirSecondNewOptimizer{} of the hundred as new-optimizer papers against the first's \nKaDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, moving \nKaDirApplicationToNewOptimizer{} papers from application to new optimizer and \nKaDirNewOptimizerToApplication{} the other way, so the first labeller's new-optimizer stratum has precision \nKaDirPrecisionVsSecond{} and recall \nKaDirRecallVsSecond{} against the second's, and under the second labeller the stratum would be roughly a third larger. The second labeller was then run over the whole corpus (\nKafPapers{} papers both labelled): the two agree on \nKafPctAgree{} (Cohen's $\kappa$ \nKafKappa{}), the second files \nKafDirSecondNewOptimizer{} papers as new-optimizer against the first's \nKafDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, and Table~\ref{tab:kinds} gives the scan rates under the second labelling and on the \nKafStBothNewOptimizerPapers{} and \nKafStBothApplicationPapers{} papers both labellers agree on; neither labelling is adjudicated by a person. The reporting gap between kinds does not depend on which labeller draws the line: on the double-labelled hundred, the papers both call new optimizers state a run count in \nKaStBothNewOptimizerRuns{} of cases and name a test in \nKaStBothNewOptimizerTest{}, the papers either calls one in \nKaStEitherNewOptimizerRuns{} and \nKaStEitherNewOptimizerTest{}, the papers both call applications in \nKaStBothApplicationRuns{} and \nKaStBothApplicationTest{}, and the \nKaStDisputedPapers{} papers the two labellers file differently in \nKaStDisputedRuns{} and \nKaStDisputedTest{}, between the two strata. On the metaphor question the two agreed on \nKaMetaphorAgree{} of \nKaMetaphorPapers{}, \nKaMetaphorNoneDisagreements{} of the disagreements being ``no'' against an undefined ``none''; the question is not used further.

\paragraph{Two readings of the text} A regular-expression scan of the full text, released with the paper, records for every paper whether it states a number of independent runs, which statistical tests it names (Wilcoxon, Friedman, $t$, Mann--Whitney, Kruskal--Wallis, ANOVA, a $p$-value, a Bonferroni or Holm correction, the $+/=/-$ convention), which benchmark suite it names (a CEC year, BBOB, the classical functions), whether it cites the methodology papers of Section~\ref{sec:related} by title, and whether it carries the journal's production note ``Significant values are in bold''. The scan is a strict-match count: it finds the phrasings it was written for and no others. A language model then read the full text of \nTrAllPapers{} papers (Section~\ref{sec:tests}) and its run-count reading is the one quoted where both exist. Table~\ref{tab:kinds} gives both by kind.

\paragraph{The plan, and what was decided after the data} The plan (released with the code) was committed to the author's repository the same day as the runs, before any corpus PDF was downloaded and after two forty-paper probes of arXiv; it is an analysis plan, not a registration with an independent registry. It fixed the unit, the test (Welch's $t$, two-sided, at the stated count, an unlabelled bar read as a deviation, $p \geq 0.05$ a failure), the corpus rule and its seven journals, the five-hundred-paper census under the frozen protocol, the four instruments and the four questions, the citing contrast among them. Decided after the data, and marked as such where they appear: the classifier's prompt and the scan's patterns (written during the fetch), the whole-text reading, the Mean/Std-row re-read instruction and the table-by-table merge rule, the means-only filter, the bold-rule sentence rescue, the all-bold-row and back-matter readings of the parser, the journal-note stratum, the genuine-holder restriction, the verdict cross-tab protocol, the shape and orientation strata, the two kits and the arXiv corpus. The review rounds that shaped these were run by models, four of them by the census reader's own model. Three of the plan's statements were not kept as written: the corpus is one journal of seven; the human labels it promised for the strata the reviewers of a companion audit had asked for were not produced for this submission (a labelling kit is released, Section~\ref{sec:validation}); and the benchmark-comparison stratum of its third question holds \nKcComparison{} papers and no comparison, so that question is not answered.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The reader census}
\label{sec:census}

\paragraph{Protocol} An image-based reader (Gemini 3.1 Pro, relay model id \texttt{gemini/gemini-3.1-pro-preview}, served through an OpenAI-compatible relay; every model reading in this paper names its model and command in the released run records) was given every page of \nCzPapersDrawn{} randomly drawn papers on which the parser had detected a table caption, \nCzPagesDrawn{} pages, rendered at twice print size with nothing marked, together with the paper's run sentences, under the protocol released with the companion audits: list every results table on the page; for each, the orientation, whether any cell prints a dispersion, what the dispersion is (SD, SE, CI, unlabelled), its layout (in the cell, in parentheses, in a separate column, in a separate row, none), the run count and where it was found; then every group with exactly one bold cell, with the bold cell's mean and dispersion and the best non-bold cell's mean and dispersion, values as printed. The one change to the protocol for this corpus is the \emph{separate row} value of the layout field. The run sentences are supplied by the text scan, so a run count ``found in the paper'' is one the scan's patterns found and the reader accepted, applied to every table of that paper; the reading whose count comes from the caption or the page alone is given below. The reader's groups are scored under the parser's unit, a group whose bold cell is not the better value in its stated direction, or ties its rival, being dropped as the parser drops it, and Welch's test is computed on the reader's values exactly as on the parser's. \nCzPagesRead{} of the \nCzPagesDrawn{} pages were read; the rest timed out at every attempt.

\paragraph{The first reading} Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the census in two columns. Under the first protocol the reader lists \nCrResultsTables{} results tables, of which \nCrTablesAnyBar{} print any dispersion, and \nCzGroups{} single-bold groups in \nCzPapersWithGroups{} papers: \nCrAllNeither{} \nCrAllNeitherCi{} print no dispersion on either side, \nCrAllOneSide{} on one side, and \nCrAllTestable{} \nCrAllTestableCi{} are testable, both dispersions on the page and a run count found. Of the \nCrAllTested{} tested groups (\nCrAllTestableN{} testable, less \nCrAllZeroBoth{} whose two deviations are both printed as zero, on which no test is defined, and \nCrAllUnreadable{} whose values the reader could not make out), held by \nCrPpPapers{} papers, \nCrAllFail{} \nCrAllFailCi{} are not separable from their rival by Welch's $t$ at the stated run count.

\paragraph{What the reader gets wrong, and the re-read} This reading has, on one layout, the blind spot the parser was set aside for. When a table prints the mean and the standard deviation as two rows (a \emph{Mean} row and a \emph{Std} row per function), the reader files the table as having no dispersion, lists the mean rows as bar-less groups and the \emph{Std} rows as further bar-less groups: it gave the \emph{separate row} value to \nCrFormSeparateRow{} of \nCrResultsTables{} results tables while the full-text reading files \nTrNewOptimizerLayoutSeparateRowPct{} of the new-optimizer papers as printing their deviation that way, and its own notes say so on the affected pages. It also lists rows labelled \emph{Std}, \emph{Rank} or \emph{Time}, which are not statistics over runs, and rows labelled \emph{Best}, \emph{Worst} or \emph{Median}, which are statistics over runs that owe no deviation, as groups when such a row happens to carry one bold cell: \nCrRowsStdRowGroups{} and \nCrRowsBestRowGroups{} groups, plus \nCrRowsNotMeansTableGroups{} groups in tables whose caption names ranks, test verdicts or settings; \nCrRowsOnlyNeither{} of the labelled rows are bar-less and \nCrRowsOnlyTested{} of them were tested. The rates over the groups that are means over runs are given as the \emph{means} rows of Table~\ref{tab:headline}.

The Mean/Std-row blind spot was repaired after the census, and the repair is reported beside it, not in place of it. Every census page holding a results table filed as having no dispersion and at least one single-bold group, \nCrrrPagesInKits{} pages in two draws (the first from the papers the full-text reading files as Mean/Std rows plus the pages whose reader notes mention a separate row, the second every other such page), was read again under the same protocol with one added instruction: to take the \emph{Std} cell beneath or beside a mean as that mean's dispersion and never to list a summary row as a group. \nCrrrPagesReread{} pages came back (\nCrrrPagesNewOptimizer{} of new-optimizer papers, \nCrrrPagesApplication{} of application papers). The re-read is merged table by table: a re-read table replaces the original only where it keeps every dispersion the original had and either lists at least as many groups of means or files a separate layout, because a re-read that returns fewer groups is a truncation, not a repair; \nCrrrTablesReplaced{} of \nCrrrTablesReread{} re-read tables replaced their original and \nCrrrTablesKept{} were refused on that rule. \paragraph{What the summaries look like} A deviation at least as large as its mean is common in benchmark tables, where the error on a solved function is a bounded, strongly right-skewed quantity near zero; on such a quantity the summary announces a shape on which a $t$-type check is poorly calibrated and the guides recommend a rank test. This stratification was made after the data. In the re-read census \nCrcShPctTestedSdGeMean{} of the tested groups have a deviation at least as large as the mean on one arm (\nCrcShSdGeMeanBoldOnlyTested{} on the bold arm only, failing \nCrcShSdGeMeanBoldOnlyFail{}; \nCrcShSdGeMeanRivalOnlyTested{} on the rival only, \nCrcShSdGeMeanRivalOnlyFail{}; \nCrcShSdGeMeanBothTested{} on both, \nCrcShSdGeMeanBothFail{}; \nCrcShBoldZeroPmZero{} bold cells are printed as $0 \pm 0$) and \nCrcShPctTestedZeroSd{} a printed deviation of exactly zero on one arm; the \nCrcShZeroBothGroups{} groups with both deviations printed as zero (\nCrcShZeroBothPapers{} papers) are the most separable of all and are counted, not tested, which is the direction unfavourable to the field: counted as passes they give \nCrcShZeroBothAsPassPctFail{} on \nCrcShZeroBothAsPassTested{}. The failure rate is \nCrcShSdGeMeanFail{} on the \nCrcShSdGeMeanTested{} groups with a deviation at least the mean on either arm and \nCrcShSdLtMeanFail{} \nCrcShSdLtMeanFailCi{} on the \nCrcShSdLtMeanTested{} with the deviation below the mean on both. A deviation at least the mean imposes no ceiling on $t$ by itself; with both arms so shaped and the quantity non-negative, $|t|$ cannot exceed $\sqrt{n}\,|m_2 - m_1| / \sqrt{m_1^2 + m_2^2}$, and that ceiling falls below the critical value in \nCrcShCeilingBinds{} tested groups (\nCrcShCeilingBindsPctTested{}), all of which fail; the rest of the stratum's excess failure is the shape, not a cap.

\paragraph{A check the run record allows} For a quantity bounded below by zero, an error to the optimum or a distance, the sample deviation over $n$ runs cannot exceed $\sqrt{n}$ times the mean, since one run can carry at most the whole sum; a raw fitness with a positive optimum is not so bounded, and the check will flag such a pair falsely, which is one more reason to read the count as approximate. Leaving aside means printed as zero, which is rounding, and summary rows, \nCrcAlImpossiblePairs{} of the re-read census's tested groups, in \nCrcAlImpossiblePairsPapers{} papers and \nCrcAlImpossiblePairsSeparate{} of them in separate-column or separate-row tables, break that bound on one arm; \nCrcAlImpossiblePairsFail{} of them fail Welch's test. Some are the reader pairing a mean with a deviation that is not its own; at least one is the authors' own printed pair, an inconsistency of the paper rather than of the reader, and the two cannot be told apart without a person. Without them the re-read census reads \nCrcAlWithoutImpossibleFail{} \nCrcAlWithoutImpossibleFailCi{} on \nCrcAlWithoutImpossibleTested{} tested groups. The check is not a bound in either direction: it misses every mis-pairing small enough to be possible, and on a raw fitness it flags a pair that is the authors' own; what it does say is that the mis-pairings it catches fail more often than the rest, which is the direction in which the ones it misses push.

\subsection{The field's own test}
\label{sec:tests}

The scan says whether a test is named; it cannot say on what. A language model read the full text of \nTrAllPapers{} distinct papers: the \nTrTestablePapers{} with a parser-testable comparison, \nTrNewOptimizerPapers{} new-optimizer papers and a seeded random \nTrRandomOtherPapers{} of the rest, \nTrOverlap{} of the first set being among the other two (one further paper returned an empty reading and is not counted), under a released prompt that asks for the run count and its sentence, the summary statistics printed, where the deviation sits, which test is reported and on what quantity, whether it is paired, the significance level, whether a per-cell verdict is printed, and what the paper says bold means (Table~\ref{tab:testreader}). \paragraph{The paper's verdict against Welch's} The plan's second question asks how Welch's verdict on the printed summaries relates to the verdict the paper's own test prints. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The convention is on the page, in a column the cell-reader cannot see} The parser's frozen headline for this corpus, \nAPctNeither{} of highlighted comparisons with no dispersion in either cell, is not the field's reporting rate; it is the rate at which the field prints its deviation \emph{inside the cell}, which is the machine-learning habit and not this one. The reader, which sees the \emph{Std} column or row once told to, finds no dispersion on either side for \nCrcAllNeither{} of its groups and for \nCrcNewOptimizerNeither{} of those in new-optimizer papers. Two lessons follow. For an audit, an instrument frozen on one literature measures a different thing on another until its blind spots are named in advance and measured afterwards; here the hazard named in advance, the \emph{Std} column, was real, and two hazards not named, a differential-evolution term of art read as a run count and reference lists read as tables, were found by the reference set and by review. For an author, the convention as practised is checkable: where the deviation and the run count are printed, a reader can recompute the comparison, and the recomputation does not separate the bold cell from its rival at the stated run count in \nCrcAllFail{} of the re-read census's tested groups, in \nCrcAllTestedPapers{} papers, the per-paper rate varying from \nCrcPpQORate{} to \nCrcPpQTRate{} across the middle half of the re-read census's papers.

\paragraph{Two literatures under one name} \nKcApplication{} of the \nSkAllPapers{} papers this query returns are applications, and by the scan they state a run count in \nSkApplicationPctRunsStated{} of cases and name a test in \nSkApplicationPctTestNamed{}, against \nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} and \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} for the papers that propose an optimizer; under the re-read census their groups are bar-less \nCrcApplicationNeither{} of the time against \nCrcNewOptimizerNeither{}. The gap survives the second labeller's line between the kinds (Section~\ref{sec:corpus}) and survives restriction to the application papers that hold a highlighted comparison (\nHkApplicationPctRunsStated{} and \nHkApplicationPctTestNamed{}). The guides are addressed to the second literature, and its run counts and test names mostly follow them; its deviations and bold legends do not (\nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} of its bold means print no deviation, \nTrNewOptimizerBoldNotStatedPct{} of its papers never say what bold means). The first, where a metaheuristic meets a scheduling problem or a network's hyperparameters, states no run count in most cases and prints the best value bold. Reviewers of application papers have the same guides available.

\paragraph{What bold means} The model's reading finds a stated rule for bold in \nTrAllBoldBestPct{} of the papers it read and none in \nTrAllBoldNotStatedPct{}; the rule tying bold to a test is, in \nTrNoteFiledStatisticallyBest{} of the \nTrNotePapersRead{} read papers that carry it, the journal's production note ``Significant values are in bold'', present under the tables of \nBnAllWithNote{} papers, \nBnAllNoteNoTestNamed{} of which name no test, so that a legend inserted by production settles nothing. Meanwhile the parser finds a whole row in bold in \nRbRowBoldPapers{} papers: in \nRbNonHeaderRowBoldPapers{} of them a row other than the header, the proposed method's row as a rule, which is a typographic claim of a different kind, and in the rest only the header row the journal's house style sets in bold. When a whole row is bold every column holds exactly one bold cell, so both instruments read such a table as column groups won by that row; the parser's \nRbRowBoldOnlyN{} such comparisons are removed in one row of Table~\ref{tab:span}, and the census is printed with and without its column-oriented tables in Section~\ref{sec:census}. The guides already say what would settle it \cite{derrac2011practical,latorre2021prescription}, and the specialist venues' papers already do it: a legend written by the authors, ``bold: best mean over $N$ runs'', with the verdict of the named test marked on each competitor's cell. This paper recommends nothing new to authors; it measures how far that practice reaches this journal's new-optimizer papers, \nTrNewOptimizerPctMarkers{} by the first reader and more by the second (Section~\ref{sec:tests}), and almost none of its applications. The one recommendation aimed elsewhere is to the publisher: a production style that inserts ``Significant values are in bold'' under the tables of \nBnAllNoteNoTestNamed{} papers that name no test would serve its readers better by requiring an author-written legend.

\paragraph{What a failing cell does not say} A bold cell that Welch's $t$ on the summaries cannot separate from its rival is not a refuted algorithm. The authors' claim is usually an aggregate, a Wilcoxon or Friedman verdict over a suite, and a per-cell failure leaves that claim where it was; what fails is the typographic claim that one printed number is the best, at the run count the authors state, on the evidence the authors chose to print. The census rate is a rate of bold cells whose printed support is thin, read with the concentration of Section~\ref{sec:census} in mind, and it is \nCrcShSdLtMeanFail{} on the cells whose deviation is below the mean on both arms.

\paragraph{The test the field names and the test the page supports} \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} of the new-optimizer papers name a statistical test, Wilcoxon and Friedman most often, as the guides ask. The page, however, prints means and deviations, which admit a $t$-type comparison and not a rank test; the Wilcoxon verdict, computed on per-run values the paper does not print, reaches the reader only as a symbol or a $p$-value where the paper prints one. That is why the audit uses Welch's $t$ on the summaries: not because it is the right test for the data, but because it is the only per-cell test the printed summaries admit, and a reader who wants to check a bold cell is in exactly that position. The printed table does admit the guides' aggregate test, a Wilcoxon signed-rank paired across functions on the per-function summaries, which \nTrNewOptimizerOnPerFunctionMeans{} of the new-optimizer papers read report using; that test asks whether the optimizer wins over the suite, not whether one cell's bold is supported, and this audit does not recompute it because the census records only the bold cell and its best rival per function. On the skewed, bounded errors that led the guides to recommend rank tests a $t$-type check on the summaries and a rank test on the runs can disagree in either direction, and for \nCrcShPctTestedSdGeMean{} of the tested groups the summary itself, a deviation at least the size of a non-negative mean, announces that shape. The per-cell test also asks a narrower question than the guides' convention: a Wilcoxon across functions on per-function summaries, or a Friedman on per-function ranks, is computable from the printed table and tests the paper's aggregate claim, which this audit does not recompute; the census rate is a statement about the bold cell, a typographic claim, and estimates what the printed summaries support for it by a $t$-type test, not what the authors' own test would find; where both verdicts are on the page (Section~\ref{sec:tests}) they agree in \nVcPctAgree{} of cases and the disagreements run towards Welch failing where the paper's rank test reports significance. The field could close this gap in either direction, by printing the test's verdict beside every bold cell, or by releasing the per-run values so that the named test can be recomputed; the guides recommend both.

\paragraph{Limitations} The corpus is the 2024--2025 metaheuristics output of one general megajournal, \nOListedSrep{} of the \nOListed{} papers the plan's query listed across \nOJournalsListed{} journals. The others were not fetched: three publishers' PDF servers answered the throttled requests with a refusal or a challenge page, and for the two journals Europe PMC mirrors (\nFaMirroredListed{} listed papers) the PDF endpoints that were tried answered a bot challenge while the bulk article-package route of PMC's FTP and cloud services was not attempted for this submission, so the one-journal corpus is in part a choice of fetch route and not only a publisher's refusal; no number here describes the venues in which this journal's readers publish, and a general-science megajournal's review may hold the convention less firmly than a specialist venue. Every stratum is by a title-and-abstract label: over the whole corpus the two labellers agree on \nKafPctAgree{} of papers, the second files \nKafDirSecondNewOptimizer{} as new-optimizer against the first's \nKafDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, Table~\ref{tab:kinds} and Section~\ref{sec:census} give the rates under both and on the papers both agree on, and neither labelling has been adjudicated by a person, so ``new-optimizer'' and ``application'' in this paper mean labelled so by the model named; the classifier's definition of a new optimizer, one evaluated against others, selects the papers that benchmark, and the full-text reading of Table~\ref{tab:kinds} covers the first labeller's stratum only. One model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) produced every reading in this paper other than the second-labeller checks: the classifier, the census, the re-read, the verdict reader and the full-text reader. The reference set measures the reader against the parser's cells, in-cell layouts only; on the separate-column and separate-row layouts, which hold \nCrcBarredGroupsSeparateLayoutPct{} of the reader's barred groups, its pairing of means with deviations is checked against a second vendor's model on two hundred kit items and against the impossible-pair check, not against a person, and the kits are released for one; the second model was handed the reader's caption, label and direction, so its agreement is a check of the values and not an independent draw. The re-read was the same model under a second instruction, merged table by table under a rule that refuses a re-read which loses a dispersion (a one-way rule: a spurious added dispersion is kept, a correctly removed one is refused), both readings are printed, and the repair reached the new-optimizer stratum more than the application stratum. Nothing checks the full-text fields against a person; the per-cell-verdict field is the one on which the two readers disagree most, every disagreement a verdict the second reader quotes and the first misses, and the \nTaMarkersNewOptimizerFirstNoSecondYes{} disputed new-optimizer papers are listed in the release for adjudication. The census protocol files a table with one whole bold row as column-oriented and the reader was not asked to tell a proposed-method row from column-wise winners; \nCrcColTested{} of the \nCrcAllTested{} tested groups sit in such tables and the rates are printed without them. The guides' aggregate test, a Wilcoxon signed-rank paired across functions, is computable from a printed table with enough function rows; the census records only the bold cell and its best rival per function, so it is not recomputed here and the per-cell rate does not test it. On the \nVcPapersLocated{} papers where a printed verdict could be located, \nVcWelchFailPrintedSignificant{} of the \nVcWelchFailLocated{} Welch failures coincide with a significant printed rank verdict; that share is the only estimate here of how much of the failure rate belongs to the test, and it is not extrapolated. The census's tested groups are concentrated in \nCrcPpPapers{} papers, three multi-objective studies holding \nCrcPpTopThreeShare{} (Kish ratio \nCrcPpEffectivePapers{}, design effect \nCrcPpDesignEffect{}); the percentile cluster bootstrap is an approximation on so few clusters (simulated coverage \nCrcPpBootCoverage{} for a nominal 95 percent, and \nCrcPpWaldCoverage{} for a cluster-robust Wald interval), intervals are withheld where the effective count is below eight, the coverage was simulated for the headline only, and the per-paper distribution and a paper-weighted rate are printed beside it. The verdict cross-tab rests on \nVcPapersLocated{} papers, three holding \nVcTopThreeShare{} of the pairs, and no interval is printed for it. The headline reading is a mixture: two-sided and unpaired at the smallest stated count push the failure rate up, an unlabelled bar read as a sample deviation pushes it down, and Table~\ref{tab:headline} and Table~\ref{tab:span} give the alternatives; the shape, orientation, note, bold-rule and citing strata were made after the data; none of the readings is a claim about what the authors would have found with their raw data. The parser's rates on this corpus measure its smallest-count rule and the format more than the evidence, and none of them is quoted as a rate of the field. The plan is a same-day analysis plan in the author's repository, not a registration, and the review rounds that shaped the paper were run by models.

\label{app:prompt}

The paper-kind classifier of Section~\ref{sec:corpus} received, for each listed paper, the text below with the title and the first three thousand characters of the abstract in place of the placeholders; it saw nothing else of the paper. The labels are those of the released code, in the order given there.

\begin{footnotesize}
\begin{verbatim}
You are classifying a paper that mentions a metaheuristic into exactly one kind.
Labels (answer with the label id only):
- new_optimizer: proposes a new metaheuristic, or a modified / improved / hybrid
  variant of one, and evaluates it against other optimizers
- comparison: compares or benchmarks existing optimizers, or studies their behaviour,
  without proposing a new one as the main contribution
- application: applies one or more metaheuristics to a domain problem (engineering
  design, scheduling, energy, medicine, machine learning tuning ...) and the
  contribution is the application
- survey_theory: a survey, review, tutorial, or a theoretical or methodological paper
  without a new optimizer
- other: anything else, or not about metaheuristics at all

Also say: is the proposed or studied optimizer named after a natural, animal, human or
  physical metaphor (e.g. grey wolf, whale, hippopotamus, political, chemical) rather
  than a mechanism (yes/no/none)? Does the abstract mention benchmark functions or a
  CEC test suite (yes/no)?

  "yes|no|none", "benchmark_suite": "yes|no"}

\title{The convention on the page: an audit of \nOListedSrep{} metaheuristics papers in \emph{Scientific Reports} against their own reporting rule}

\author[1]{Author name\corref{cor1}}

\begin{abstract}
Metaheuristics has an explicit reporting convention: a fixed number of independent runs, thirty by tradition, a mean and standard deviation per function, the best value in bold, a non-parametric test. We ask how much of it reaches the printed page, on every 2024--2025 article of \emph{Scientific Reports} that OpenAlex lists as mentioning a metaheuristic (\nOListedSrep{} papers), with instruments fixed before download and released with every run record: a model reading of title and abstract sorting the papers into \nKcNewOptimizer{} proposing an optimizer and \nKcApplication{} applying one, a scan and a model reading of the full text, an image census of every table page of \nCzPapersDrawn{} random papers, and a text-layer parser frozen on machine-learning tables. The convention lives in the new-optimizer papers: \nTrNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} of those read state a run count and \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} name a test, against \nTrRandomOtherPctRunsStated{} and \nSkApplicationPctTestNamed{} for applications. In the census, \nCrNewOptimizerNeither{} of the bold cells in new-optimizer papers have no deviation on either side, against \nCrApplicationNeither{} in application papers; the deviation, where printed, sits in a separate column a cell-level parser cannot see, so the parser can test \nAPctTestable{} of its comparisons and the reader \nCzTestable{}. Of the \nCzReaderFailN{} bold cells the reader can test, in \nCrppPapers{} papers, \nCzReaderFailPct{} are not separable from their rival by Welch's $t$ on the printed summaries at the stated run count. Only \nTrNewOptimizerPctMarkers{} of the new-optimizer papers print their own test's verdict beside the table; \nTrAllBoldNotStatedPct{} of the papers read never say what bold means, and the one rule tying bold to a test is the journal's production note. The reader's precision on the separate-column layout is not yet measured by a person; the kit for it is released. Two companion audits of other venues are under review elsewhere.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A results table in a metaheuristics paper is a promise with a shape. Each row is a benchmark function, each column an optimizer; the cell holds the mean over some number of independent runs, with the standard deviation beside it or on the line below; the best mean in the row is bold; a Wilcoxon signed-rank or Friedman test, somewhere later, says whether the bold is more than noise. The shape is not an accident. It was argued into the field's habits by García et al.\ \cite{garcia2009study,garcia2010advanced} and Derrac et al.\ \cite{derrac2011practical}, restated in every methodological guide since \cite{carrasco2020recent,latorre2021prescription,osaba2021tutorial,bartz2020benchmarking,beiranvand2017best,kendall2016good}, and enforced, to a degree, by the competitions and platforms that fix the run count, the budget and the summary statistics: the CEC special sessions and, for BBOB, COCO \cite{hansen2021coco}.

What the guides do not say is how often the shape survives contact with a page. A bold cell is only checkable if the reader can see both means, both deviations and the run count; a test is only a test if the paper says which one, on what, and paired or not. This paper measures those things on the page, with instruments that are fixed before the corpus is downloaded and released with it, so that the measurement is reproducible and its errors are stated rather than guessed at.

The measurement is deliberately narrow. It does not re-run any optimizer, does not ask whether a proposed algorithm is new (a question others have taken up \cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics,aranha2022metaphor,camacho2022exposing,tzanetos2021nature,piotrowski2018some}), and does not judge whether a benchmark suite is the right one \cite{kudela2022critical}. It asks only: given the numbers the authors chose to print, does the bold cell stand at the run count the authors state, and does the paper's own test say the same thing? Where the printed numbers cannot answer, that is the finding.

The paper makes three contributions. First, a census of reporting practice over the \nOParsed{} metaheuristics papers that one open-access megajournal, \emph{Scientific Reports}, published in 2024--2025, by instruments whose precision and coverage are measured rather than assumed: how often a bold cell has a deviation and a run count on the page, how often it survives Welch's $t$ on the printed numbers, and how those rates split between papers that propose an optimizer, papers that apply one, and papers that compare several. Second, a reading of the field's own statistical convention beside the printed evidence: which tests are named, at what run count, and whether papers that cite the methodology literature report differently. Third, the released instruments, protocols and run records, so that every number here can be regenerated and the corpus rule changed by anyone who disagrees with it.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Methodology guides} The statistical convention of the field is unusually well documented. García et al.\ \cite{garcia2009study} argued from the CEC 2005 special session that parametric tests are inappropriate for optimizer comparisons and recommended the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for pairs and the Friedman test with post-hoc procedures for groups; Derrac et al.\ \cite{derrac2011practical} turned that into a tutorial in the first issue of this journal, and Carrasco et al.\ \cite{carrasco2020recent} surveyed a decade of its uptake, finding that non-parametric tests had become common while their assumptions were rarely checked. LaTorre et al.\ \cite{latorre2021prescription} and Osaba et al.\ \cite{osaba2021tutorial} give prescriptive guidelines covering the run count, the benchmark suite, the tuning of competitors and the test; Bartz-Beielstein et al.\ \cite{bartz2020benchmarking}, Beiranvand et al.\ \cite{beiranvand2017best}, Kendall et al.\ \cite{kendall2016good} and, earlier, Hooker \cite{hooker1995testing} and Eiben and Jelasity \cite{eiben2002critical} make the same case from the benchmarking and operational-research sides. Our study reads the literature that those guides address and reports, paper by paper, what of the guidance reached print.

\paragraph{Critiques of the field} A second literature asks whether new metaheuristics are new. Sörensen \cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics} called the metaphor-driven proposals of the 2000s a step backwards; Aranha et al.\ \cite{aranha2022metaphor} issued a call for action signed by editors of several journals; Camacho-Villalón et al.\ \cite{camacho2022exposing} showed six widely cited algorithms to be re-descriptions of older ones; Molina et al.\ \cite{molina2020comprehensive}, Tzanetos and Dounias \cite{tzanetos2021nature}, Velasco et al.\ \cite{velasco2024literature} and Campelo and Aranha \cite{campelo2023lessons} catalogue and taxonomise the population. Kudela \cite{kudela2022critical} showed that a common benchmark hazard, functions whose optimum lies at the origin, lets centre-biased algorithms appear to win. Our question is orthogonal to novelty and to benchmark choice: it concerns whether the evidence printed for a claim, whatever the algorithm and the suite, can be checked.

\paragraph{Audits of reporting} Statcheck \cite{nuijten2016prevalence} recomputed $p$-values from printed test statistics in psychology and found inconsistencies in half of the papers; AxCell \cite{kardas2020axcell} extracted results tables from machine-learning papers into leaderboards. The instruments here belong to that family, read the tables rather than the prose, and were first applied to two machine-learning and multi-agent venues in companion audits that are under review; this is their third application and the first to a field with an explicit convention to audit against. The parser is unchanged, and Section~\ref{sec:parser} says what that costs on this format; what is new is the corpus, the reader census as the quoted instrument, the reading of the field's own tests, and the classification by kind of paper.

\section{What is measured}
\label{sec:what}

\paragraph{The unit} A \emph{comparison} is one bold cell in a results table set against the best non-bold cell in its group, the group being the row or the column within which the table marks its winners. The parser decides the orientation per table by which axis carries exactly one bold cell per line, and reads the direction (lower is better for an error, higher for a hit rate) from the header. Tables where bold marks several cells per line, where the caption announces first, second and third best, and cells tied with their rival, are left out and counted.

\paragraph{Testability} A comparison is \emph{testable} when both cells carry a dispersion and a run count is available: from the caption, from the page, or, failing both, the smallest count stated anywhere in the paper. On the machine-learning tables the parser was frozen on, the smallest count is the reading least favourable to the claim; on this corpus it is sometimes not a run count at all (Section~\ref{sec:parser}). An unlabelled dispersion is read as a standard deviation, the reading that makes the claim easiest to support; a bracketed pair is read as an interval of the mean and tested in the normal limit without a run count.

\paragraph{The test} On a testable comparison we compute Welch's $t$ \cite{welch1947generalization} from the two means, the two deviations and the run count, with the same count on both arms, and call the comparison \emph{failing} when $p \geq 0.05$ two-sided. A failure says that the printed summaries do not separate the bold cell from its rival at the stated run count by a $t$-type test; it does not say that the claim is false, it does not say what the authors' own test would find on the per-run values, and it says nothing about a difference the authors never asserted. Many tables bold the best mean by convention; for those, a failure measures how thin the printed evidence is. Holm's correction \cite{holm1979simple} within a table is reported beside the raw rate.

\paragraph{The format hazard} Metaheuristics tables often print the mean and the standard deviation as two rows (\emph{Mean} / \emph{Std}) or two columns of the same table rather than as $a \pm b$ in one cell. The parser, frozen before this corpus was chosen, reads such a mean as bar-less. This was stated in the pre-registered plan before any page was read, and the image-based reader census of Section~\ref{sec:census} is therefore the instrument this paper quotes for the presence of dispersions; its protocol gains one value of one field, recording whether the dispersion sits beside the mean, in a separate row, in a separate column, or nowhere.

\paragraph{Frozen} The parser and the rules above were fixed and tagged before the first companion audit and have not been changed since; this study adds readings on its output, not changes to it, and Section~\ref{sec:parser} says what those readings found.

\section{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

The plan fixed, before any download, a corpus of fully open-access CC-BY journals with a metaheuristics output, selected by an API query so that the corpus is reproducible: every article of 2024 or 2025 whose title or abstract matches a phrase list (metaheuristic, evolutionary algorithm, genetic algorithm, particle swarm, differential evolution, swarm intelligence, ant colony, simulated annealing, nature-inspired, bio-inspired optimization, grey wolf, whale optimization, memetic, NSGA) in OpenAlex.\footnote{\url{https://openalex.org}; the query and the list are in the released code.} Hybrid journals, this one included, were excluded because their open-access subset is a selected sample. The query listed \nOListed{} papers across \nOJournalsListed{} journals. Three publishers refused automated download of their PDFs (a bot challenge, a rate limit and a proxy block; the attempts are in the released fetch log, and throttled re-attempts and the publishers' own APIs were not tried), and the corpus is therefore the \emph{Scientific Reports} subset: \nOListedSrep{} papers (\nOListedSrepTwentyFour{} of 2024 and \nOListedSrepTwentyFive{} of 2025), \nOFetched{} fetched and \nOParsed{} parsed. This is a deviation from the plan: the corpus is one megajournal's metaheuristics output rather than seven journals', and no number in this paper describes the venues in which this journal's readers publish.

\paragraph{Two literatures} A language model read the title and abstract of each listed paper and assigned one kind: \emph{new optimizer} (a new, modified or hybrid metaheuristic evaluated against others), \emph{comparison} (existing optimizers benchmarked, none proposed), \emph{application} (one or more metaheuristics applied to a domain problem, the application being the contribution), \emph{survey or theory}, or \emph{other}; the prompt asked also whether the optimizer is metaphor-named and whether the abstract mentions benchmark functions. By this reading the corpus is \nKcApplication{} application papers, \nKcNewOptimizer{} new-optimizer papers, \nKcComparison{} comparisons, \nKcSurveyTheory{} surveys and \nKcOther{} others. The ratio is a property of matching the phrase list against a general-science journal's abstracts, not of the field, and the two kinds report differently enough that every rate below is given by kind. A second vendor's model labelled a random \nKaPapers{} papers under the same prompt and agreed with the first on \nKaAgree{} (\nKaPctAgree{}, Wilson \nKaCiAgree{}; Cohen's $\kappa$ \nKaKappa{}); the disagreements run one way, the second labeller filing \nKaDirSecondNewOptimizer{} of the hundred as new-optimizer papers against the first's \nKaDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, moving \nKaDirApplicationToNewOptimizer{} papers from application to new optimizer and \nKaDirNewOptimizerToApplication{} the other way, so the first labeller's new-optimizer stratum has precision \nKaDirPrecisionVsSecond{} and recall \nKaDirRecallVsSecond{} against the second's, and under the second labeller the stratum would be roughly a third larger. The reporting gap between kinds does not depend on which labeller draws the line: on the double-labelled hundred, the papers both call new optimizers state a run count in \nKaStBothNewOptimizerRuns{} of cases and name a test in \nKaStBothNewOptimizerTest{}, the papers either calls one in \nKaStEitherNewOptimizerRuns{} and \nKaStEitherNewOptimizerTest{}, and the papers both call applications in \nKaStBothApplicationRuns{} and \nKaStBothApplicationTest{}. On the metaphor question the two agreed on \nKaMetaphorAgree{} of \nKaMetaphorPapers{}, \nKaMetaphorNoneDisagreements{} of the disagreements being ``no'' against an undefined ``none''; the question is not used further.

\paragraph{Two readings of the text} A regular-expression scan of the full text, released with the paper, records for every paper whether it states a number of independent runs, which statistical tests it names (Wilcoxon, Friedman, $t$, Mann--Whitney, Kruskal--Wallis, ANOVA, a $p$-value, a Bonferroni or Holm correction, the $+/=/-$ convention), which benchmark suite it names (a CEC year, BBOB, the classical functions), whether it cites the methodology papers of Section~\ref{sec:related} by title, and whether it carries the journal's production note ``Significant values are in bold''. The scan is a lower bound: it finds the phrasings it was written for. A language model then read the full text of \nTrAllPapers{} papers (Section~\ref{sec:tests}) and its run-count reading is the one quoted where both exist. Table~\ref{tab:kinds} gives both by kind.

\paragraph{Departures from the plan} The plan (released with the code) was written before any corpus PDF was downloaded but after two forty-paper probes of arXiv, and is dated the same day as the runs. Three of its statements were not kept as written: the corpus is one journal of seven; the human labels it promised for the strata the reviewers of a companion audit had asked for were not produced for this submission (a labelling kit is released, Section~\ref{sec:validation}); and the benchmark-comparison stratum of its third question holds \nKcComparison{} papers and no comparison, so that question is not answered.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The reader census}
\label{sec:census}

\paragraph{Protocol} An image-based reader (a vision-language model, named with its version in the released run records) was given every page of \nCzPapersDrawn{} randomly drawn papers on which the parser had detected a table caption, \nCzPagesDrawn{} pages, rendered at twice print size with nothing marked, together with the paper's run sentences, under the protocol released with the companion audits: list every results table on the page; for each, the orientation, whether any cell prints a dispersion, what the dispersion is (SD, SE, CI, unlabelled), its layout (in the cell, in parentheses, in a separate column, in a separate row, none), the run count and where it was found; then every group with exactly one bold cell, with the bold cell's mean and dispersion and the best non-bold cell's mean and dispersion, values as printed. The one change to the protocol for this corpus is the \emph{separate row} value of the layout field. The reader's groups are scored under the parser's unit, a group whose bold cell is not the better value in its stated direction, or ties its rival, being dropped as the parser drops it, and Welch's test is computed on the reader's values exactly as on the parser's. \nCzPagesRead{} of the \nCzPagesDrawn{} pages were read; the rest timed out at every attempt.

\paragraph{What the census reads} Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the census beside the parser. The reader lists \nCzTables{} results tables, of which \nCzTablesWithBar{} print any dispersion; the layout is \emph{none} for \nCrFormNonePct{} of them, a separate column or row for \nCrFormSeparatePct{}, a $\pm$ or a parenthesis in the cell for \nCrFormPmPct{} and \nCrFormParenPct{}. It finds \nCzGroups{} single-bold groups in \nCzPapersWithGroups{} papers: \nCzNeither{} \nCzNeitherCi{} print no dispersion on either side, \nCzOneSide{} on one side, and \nCzTestable{} \nCzTestableCi{} are testable, both dispersions on the page and a run count found. \nCrBarredGroupsSeparateLayoutPct{} of the groups with both dispersions sit in tables whose dispersion is a separate column or row, the layout the parser cannot read: this is the format hazard the plan named, measured. Of the \nCzReaderFailN{} tested groups (\nCzTestableN{} testable, less those whose values the reader could not make out), held by \nCrppPapers{} papers, \nCzReaderFailPct{} \nCzReaderFailCi{} are not separable from their rival by Welch's $t$ at the stated run count.

\paragraph{Where the tested groups sit} The tested groups are concentrated: four papers hold \nCrppTopFourShare{} of them and ten hold \nCrppTopTenShare{}; the largest holds \nCrppLargestPaperTested{} and fails \nCrppLargestPaperFail{} of them. Across the \nCrppPapers{} papers the per-paper failure share has median \nCrppMedianRate{} (quartiles \nCrppQORate{} and \nCrppQTRate{}), \nCrppPapersAnyFail{} papers have at least one failing group, the rate without the largest paper is \nCrppWithoutLargestFail{} \nCrppWithoutLargestFailCi{} on \nCrppWithoutLargestTested{}, and without the four largest \nCrppWithoutTopFourFail{} \nCrppWithoutTopFourFailCi{} on \nCrppWithoutTopFourTested{}. The cluster bootstrap over papers is valid under this concentration but does not display it, which is why the distribution is printed.

\paragraph{What the summaries look like} A deviation at least as large as its mean, or a mean below $10^{-3}$, is common in benchmark tables, where the error on a solved function is a heavy-tailed quantity near zero. \nCrshPctTestedSdGeMean{} of the tested groups have a deviation at least as large as the mean on one arm, and \nCrshPctTestedZeroSd{} a printed deviation of exactly zero. The failure rate is \nCrshSdGeMeanFail{} on the \nCrshSdGeMeanTested{} groups with a deviation at least the mean, \nCrshMeanTinyFail{} on the \nCrshMeanTinyTested{} with a mean below $10^{-3}$, \nCrshEitherFail{} on the \nCrshEitherTested{} with either, and \nCrshNeitherFail{} \nCrshNeitherFailCi{} on the \nCrshNeitherTested{} with neither. For a fifth of the tested groups the summary itself says that no $t$-type test fits; those are the groups the field's non-parametric convention exists for, and they fail Welch's test more often, not less.

\paragraph{What the reader gets wrong} The reader tells a separate \emph{Std} row from a separate column unreliably: on the papers the full-text reading labels as Mean/Std rows it files most tables as separate-column or as none, and its notes say so. It also lists rows labelled \emph{Std}, \emph{Rank}, \emph{Best} or \emph{Worst} as groups when such a row happens to carry one bold cell: \nCrrowGroups{} groups (\nCrrowPct{}) carry such a label, every one of them bar-less; without them the census reads \nCrrowWithoutNeither{} bar-less and \nCrrowWithoutTestable{} testable, and the tested set is unchanged. The reader's precision on the in-cell layouts is measured on the reference set of Section~\ref{sec:validation}; on the separate-column layout, which holds most of the tested groups, it is not yet measured by anyone, and the same section says what was prepared to measure it.

\paragraph{The run count} The reader finds a run count for \nCrTablesWithRunCount{} of the results tables, from the caption in \nCrNSourceCaption{} cases, the page in \nCrNSourcePage{} and the run sentences of the paper in \nCrNSourcePaper{}; the modal count is \nCrRunsRankOneN{} (\nCrRunsRankOneCount{} tables) and \nCrTablesAtThirty{} of the tables are read at thirty. Where the paper's text scan also states a count, the reader's agrees on \nCrNAgreesScan{} tested groups and disagrees on \nCrNDisagreesScan{}.

\subsection{By kind of paper}
\label{sec:kinds}

Table~\ref{tab:kinds} splits everything by kind. The new-optimizer papers are where the convention lives: \nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} state a run count by the scan and \nTrNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} by the model reading, with thirty the count in \nSkNewOptimizerPctThirtyModal{} and \nTrNewOptimizerPctRunsThirty{} respectively; \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} name a statistical test (Wilcoxon in \nSkNewOptimizerWilcoxon{} papers, Friedman in \nSkNewOptimizerFriedman{}); \nSkNewOptimizerPctCec{} name a CEC suite; \nSkNewOptimizerPctCitesMethodology{} cite one of the methodology guides. \subsection{The field's own test}
\label{sec:tests}

The scan says whether a test is named; it cannot say on what. \subsection{What the frozen parser saw, and why}
\label{sec:parser}

The parser detected \nATablesDetected{} table captions in the \nOParsed{} papers and read \nAComparisons{} highlighted comparisons from \nATablesYielding{} of them, in \nAClaimPapers{} papers. \nAPctNeitherCi{} of those print no dispersion in either cell and \nAPctTestableCi{} are testable, \nANTestable{} comparisons in \nAPapersTestable{} papers, of which \nAPctFailCi{} fail Welch's test at the parser's run count. These are the pre-registered numbers, and on this corpus they measure the instrument more than the field, in three ways that the reference set and review exposed and that the readings of Table~\ref{tab:span} remove one at a time. First, the layout: the parser reads a dispersion only inside the cell, and the reader finds \nCrBarredGroupsSeparateLayoutPct{} of the groups with both dispersions in separate-column tables. Second, the bold: this journal's house style sets header rows in bold, and many papers set the proposed method's whole row in bold; a header row holding numbers (a dimension, a year, a parameter value) becomes a column of winners, and a proposed-method row becomes a claim of a different kind. \nRbRowBoldOnlyN{} of the \nAComparisons{} comparisons, in \nRbRowBoldPapers{} papers, have their bold cell in a row whose every numeric cell is bold, \nRbHeaderRowOnlyN{} of them in the table's first row; and \nPrBackMatterAllComparisons{} comparisons come from the back matter of a paper, where a reference-list line, a volume number with its issue in parentheses against a page number with its year in parentheses, parses as a mean with a deviation. \nPrBackMatterOnlyN{} of the \nANTestable{} tested comparisons, in \nPrBackMatterPapers{} papers, are such lines. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The convention is on the page, in a column the cell-reader cannot see} The parser's pre-registered headline for this corpus, \nAPctNeither{} of highlighted comparisons with no dispersion in either cell, is not the field's reporting rate; it is the rate at which the field prints its deviation \emph{inside the cell}, which is the machine-learning habit and not this one. The reader, which sees the \emph{Std} column, finds a dispersion on both sides for \nCzTestable{} of its groups and for \nCrNewOptimizerTestable{} of those in new-optimizer papers. Two lessons follow. For an audit, an instrument frozen on one literature measures a different thing on another until its blind spots are named in advance and measured afterwards; here the hazard named in advance, the \emph{Std} column, was real, and two hazards not named, a differential-evolution term of art read as a run count and reference lists read as tables, were found by the reference set and by review. For an author, the convention as practised is checkable: where the deviation and the run count are printed, a reader can recompute the comparison, and the recomputation does not separate the bold cell from its rival at the stated run count in \nCzReaderFailPct{} of the reader's tested groups, in \nCrppPapers{} papers, the rate varying from \nCrppQORate{} to \nCrppQTRate{} across the middle half of those papers.

\paragraph{Two literatures under one name} \nKcApplication{} of the \nSkAllPapers{} papers this query returns are applications, and by the scan they state a run count in \nSkApplicationPctRunsStated{} of cases and name a test in \nSkApplicationPctTestNamed{}, against \nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} and \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} for the papers that propose an optimizer; under the reader their groups are bar-less \nCrApplicationNeither{} of the time against \nCrNewOptimizerNeither{}. The gap survives the second labeller's line between the kinds (Section~\ref{sec:corpus}) and survives restriction to the application papers that hold a highlighted comparison (\nHkApplicationPctRunsStated{} and \nHkApplicationPctTestNamed{}). The guides are addressed to the second literature and are mostly followed there. The first, where a metaheuristic meets a scheduling problem or a network's hyperparameters, states no run count in most cases and prints the best value bold. Reviewers of application papers have the same guides available.

\paragraph{What bold means} The model's reading finds a stated rule for bold in \nTrAllBoldBestPct{} of the papers it read and none in \nTrAllBoldNotStatedPct{}; the only rule tying bold to a test is the journal's production note ``Significant values are in bold'', present under the tables of \nBnAllWithNote{} papers, some of which name no test, so that a legend inserted by production settles nothing. Meanwhile \nRbRowBoldPapers{} papers set a whole row in bold, a typographic claim of a different kind, and the journal's house style sets header rows in bold. The guides already say what would settle it \cite{derrac2011practical,latorre2021prescription}: a legend written by the authors, ``bold: best mean over $N$ runs'', with the verdict of the named test marked on each competitor's cell; \nTrNewOptimizerPctMarkers{} of the new-optimizer papers print such a per-cell verdict, \nTrNewOptimizerPctMarkersAmongNamed{} of those that name a test.

\paragraph{The test the field names and the test the page supports} \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} of the new-optimizer papers name a statistical test, Wilcoxon and Friedman most often, as the guides ask. The page, however, prints means and deviations, which admit a $t$-type comparison and not a rank test; the Wilcoxon verdict, computed on per-run values the paper does not print, reaches the reader only as a symbol or a $p$-value where the paper prints one. That is why the audit uses Welch's $t$ on the summaries: not because it is the right test for the data, but because it is the only test the printed summaries admit, and a reader who wants to check a bold cell is in exactly that position. On the heavy-tailed errors that led the guides to recommend rank tests a $t$-test rejects less often than a Wilcoxon on the same runs, and for \nCrshPctTestedSdGeMean{} of the tested groups the summary itself, a deviation at least the size of a non-negative mean, says that no $t$-type test fits. The census rate is therefore a bound on what the printed summaries support by a $t$-type test, not an estimate of what the authors' own test would find. The field could close this gap in either direction, by printing the test's verdict beside every bold cell, or by releasing the per-run values so that the named test can be recomputed; the guides recommend both.

\paragraph{Limitations} The corpus is the 2024--2025 metaheuristics output of one megajournal, \nOListedSrep{} of the \nOListed{} papers the pre-registered query listed across \nOJournalsListed{} journals; the others were not fetched because their publishers blocked automated download, and no number here describes the venues in which this journal's readers publish. The five-to-one ratio of application to new-optimizer papers is a property of the query against a general-science journal, and the new-optimizer stratum is \nKcNewOptimizer{} papers by one labeller and about a third more by the other. The readers are models: the reference set measures the reader against the parser's cells, in-cell layouts only, and against a second vendor's model, not against a person; on the separate-column layout, which holds \nCrBarredGroupsSeparateLayoutPct{} of the reader's barred groups, its precision is unmeasured and the kit to measure it is released; the two model readers agree on the rival's mean in \nAgRunnerMeanFrac{} reference items and on the run count in \nAgNRunsFrac{}, and no error from that disagreement is propagated into the census intervals. The full-text reading of \nTrAllPapers{} papers is one model's under a released prompt; its run count agrees with the text scan where both state one, but its per-cell-verdict and bold-rule fields are checked against a second vendor's model on \nTaPapers{} papers and not against a person, and of the \nTrNotePapersRead{} read papers that carry the journal's production note the model filed \nTrNoteFiledNotStated{} as stating no bold rule and \nTrNoteFiledStatisticallyBest{} as tying bold to a test. The census's tested groups are concentrated in \nCrppPapers{} papers, four of which hold \nCrppTopFourShare{}; the cluster bootstrap is valid under that concentration, and the per-paper distribution is printed so that the reader can see it. The parser's rates on this corpus measure its smallest-count rule and the format more than the evidence, and none of them is quoted as a rate of the field. Welch's $t$ on the printed summaries is a conservative reading of what the page supports and not the field's test, and Table~\ref{tab:span} gives the alternatives; none of them is a claim about what the authors would have found with their raw data.

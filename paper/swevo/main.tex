\title{The convention on the page: an audit of \nOListedSrep{} metaheuristics papers in \emph{Scientific Reports} against their own reporting rule}

\author[1]{Author name\corref{cor1}}

\begin{abstract}
Metaheuristics has a reporting convention: independent runs, a mean and standard deviation per function, the best in bold, a non-parametric test. We audit how much reaches the page in the metaheuristics output of 2024 and 2025 in one megajournal, \emph{Scientific Reports} (\nOListedSrep{} papers), with released model instruments and a plan committed as the fetch began. The convention belongs to papers that propose an optimizer: by a model reading \nTrNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} state a run count and \nTrNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} name a test, against \nTrRandomOtherPctRunsStated{} and \nTrRandomOtherPctTestNamed{} of a random hundred of the rest, mostly applications; by text scan the gap holds under a second labeller and in two further journals. All four parts of the convention are met by \nTrNewOptimizerFullConvention{} of the \nTrNewOptimizerPapers{} new-optimizer papers read (\nTaConventionNewOptimizerFirstPct{} against \nTaConventionNewOptimizerSecondPct{} by the two readers on the papers both cover, the second finding more printed verdicts). In an image census of five hundred random papers, \nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} \nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeitherCi{} of the bold means in new-optimizer papers have no deviation printed on their table, though only \nTrNewOptimizerLayoutNonePct{} of the papers read print none anywhere; where one is printed it sits in a separate row or column three times in four. Of the \nCrcMeansTested{} testable bold means, in \nCrcPpmPapers{} papers, three multi-objective studies holding \nCrcPpmTopThreeShare{}, \nCrcMeansFail{} \nCrcMeansFailCi{} lead their rival by less than Welch's $t$ resolves at the stated run count (median paper \nCrcPpmMedianRate{}), a margin the guides never ask for; of the \nVcWelchFailLocated{} such cells beside a printed verdict, \nVcWelchFailPrintedSignificant{} are called significant by the paper's own rank test. The publisher's note ``Significant values are in bold'' sits under tables in \nBnAllWithNote{} papers, \nBnAllNoteNoTestNamed{} of which name no test. The reader's pairing of means with deviations is checked against a second model on two hundred released kit items, not yet against a person.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A results table in a metaheuristics paper is a promise with a shape. Each row is a benchmark function, each column an optimizer; the cell holds the mean over some number of independent runs, with the standard deviation beside it or on the line below; the best mean in the row is bold; a Wilcoxon signed-rank or Friedman test, somewhere later, says whether the bold is more than noise. The shape is not an accident. It was argued into the field's habits by García et al.\ \cite{garcia2009study,garcia2010advanced} and Derrac et al.\ \cite{derrac2011practical}, restated in every methodological guide since \cite{carrasco2020recent,latorre2021prescription,osaba2021tutorial,bartz2020benchmarking,beiranvand2017best,kendall2016good}, and enforced, to a degree, by the competitions and platforms that fix the run count, the budget and the summary statistics: the CEC special sessions and, for BBOB, COCO \cite{hansen2021coco}.

What the guides do not say is how often the shape survives contact with a page. A bold cell is only checkable if the reader can see both means, both deviations and the run count; a test is only a test if the paper says which one, on what, and paired or not. This paper measures those things on the page, with instruments that are fixed before the corpus is downloaded and released with it, so that the measurement is reproducible and its errors are stated rather than guessed at.

The measurement is deliberately narrow. It does not re-run any optimizer, does not ask whether a proposed algorithm is new (a question others have taken up \cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics,aranha2022metaphor,camacho2022exposing,tzanetos2021nature,piotrowski2018some}), and does not judge whether a benchmark suite is the right one \cite{kudela2022critical}. It asks only: given the numbers the authors chose to print, does the bold cell stand at the run count the authors state, and does the paper's own test say the same thing? The bold cell is a typographic marker of the best mean on one function. Most new-optimizer papers that name a test run it on the per-run values of each function (\nTrNewOptimizerOnPerRunValues{} of \nTrNewOptimizerTestNamed{}, by the full-text reading of Section~\ref{sec:tests}); a minority pair the functions and test across them (\nTrNewOptimizerOnPerFunctionMeans{}). The per-cell check here asks the first question with a different statistic on coarser evidence, and does not ask the second. Where the printed numbers cannot answer, that is the finding.

The paper makes three contributions. First, a census of reporting practice over the \nOListedSrep{} metaheuristics papers that one open-access megajournal, \emph{Scientific Reports}, published in 2024 and 2025, by instruments whose precision and coverage are measured rather than assumed: how often a bold cell has a deviation and a run count on the page, how often it survives Welch's $t$ on the printed numbers, and how those rates split between papers that propose an optimizer, papers that apply one, and papers that compare several. Second, a reading of the field's own statistical convention beside the printed evidence: which tests are named, at what run count, and whether papers that cite the methodology literature report differently. Third, the release: every instrument with the protocol it ran under, and the run record behind every number here, so that a reader who disagrees with the corpus rule can change it and recompute.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Methodology guides} The statistical convention of the field is unusually well documented. García et al.\ \cite{garcia2009study} argued from the CEC 2005 special session that parametric tests are inappropriate for optimizer comparisons and recommended the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for pairs and the Friedman test with post-hoc procedures for groups; Derrac et al.\ \cite{derrac2011practical} turned that into a tutorial in the first issue of this journal, and Carrasco et al.\ \cite{carrasco2020recent} surveyed a decade of its uptake and the misuses that came with it. LaTorre et al.\ \cite{latorre2021prescription} and Osaba et al.\ \cite{osaba2021tutorial} give prescriptive guidelines covering the run count, the benchmark suite, the tuning of competitors and the test; Bartz-Beielstein et al.\ \cite{bartz2020benchmarking}, Beiranvand et al.\ \cite{beiranvand2017best}, Kendall et al.\ \cite{kendall2016good} and, earlier, Hooker \cite{hooker1995testing} and Eiben and Jelasity \cite{eiben2002critical} make the same case from the benchmarking and operational-research sides. Our study reads the literature that those guides address and reports, paper by paper, what of the guidance reached print.

\paragraph{Critiques of the field} A second literature asks whether new metaheuristics are new. Sörensen \cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics} argued that the metaphor-driven proposals of the 2000s were leading the field away from scientific rigour; Aranha et al.\ \cite{aranha2022metaphor} issued a call for action against them; Camacho-Villalón et al.\ \cite{camacho2022exposing} showed six widely cited algorithms to be re-descriptions of older ones; Molina et al.\ \cite{molina2020comprehensive}, Velasco et al.\ \cite{velasco2024literature} and Campelo and Aranha \cite{campelo2023lessons} catalogue and taxonomise the population, and Tzanetos and Dounias \cite{tzanetos2021nature} ask how many of its members are variations of older metaheuristics. Kudela \cite{kudela2022critical} showed that a common benchmark hazard, functions whose optimum lies at the origin, lets centre-biased algorithms appear to win. Our question is orthogonal to novelty and to benchmark choice: it concerns whether the evidence printed for a claim, whatever the algorithm and the suite, can be checked.

\paragraph{Audits of reporting} Statcheck \cite{nuijten2016prevalence} recomputed $p$-values from printed test statistics in psychology and found inconsistencies in half of the papers; AxCell \cite{kardas2020axcell} extracted results tables from machine-learning papers into leaderboards. The instruments here belong to that family, read the tables rather than the prose, and were first applied to two machine-learning and multi-agent venues in companion audits that are under review; this is their third application and the first to a field with an explicit convention to audit against. The parser is unchanged, and the Supplement (Section~S1) says what that costs on this format; what is new is the corpus, the reader census as the quoted instrument, the reading of the field's own tests, and the classification by kind of paper.

\section{What is measured}
\label{sec:what}

\paragraph{The unit} A \emph{comparison} is one bold cell in a results table set against the best non-bold cell in its group, the group being the row or the column within which the table marks its winners. The parser decides the orientation per table by which axis carries exactly one bold cell per line, and reads the direction (lower is better for an error, higher for a hit rate) from the header. Tables where bold marks several cells per line, where the caption announces the three best values, and cells tied with their rival, are left out and counted.

\paragraph{Testability} A comparison is \emph{testable} when both cells carry a dispersion and a run count is available: from the caption, from the page, or, failing both, the smallest count stated anywhere in the paper. That is the parser's rule; the census takes the count from the reader's per-table field, the caption, the page or the paper's run sentences, and no census group is tested without one. On the machine-learning tables the parser was frozen on, the smallest count is the reading least favourable to separating the cells, which raises the unresolved rate; on this corpus it is sometimes not a run count at all, and moving to the text scan's count takes the parser's rate from \nPrAsReportedFail{} to \nPrAsReportedScanFail{} (Supplement, Section~S1). An unlabelled dispersion is read as a standard deviation, the reading that makes the claim easiest to support; a bracketed pair is read by the parser as an interval of the mean and tested in the normal limit without a run count; no census group is tested that way (on this corpus a bracketed pair under a mean is often the best and worst run, which is not an interval of the mean; the reader files \nCrcFormBracket{} results tables that way and the parser's version affects a handful of cells in the Supplement's Table~S1).

\paragraph{The test} On a testable comparison we compute Welch's $t$ \cite{welch1947generalization} from the two means, the two deviations and the run count, with the same count on both arms since the paper states one count for all its optimizers (where the arms in fact differed, the statistic and its degrees of freedom would too), and call the comparison \emph{unresolved} when $p \geq 0.05$ two-sided; the word failure is avoided, since no norm of the field is breached by such a cell. An unresolved comparison says that the printed summaries do not separate the bold cell from its rival at the stated run count by a $t$-type test; it does not say that the claim is false, it does not say what the authors' own test would find on the per-run values or across functions, and it says nothing about a difference the authors never asserted. Many tables bold the best mean by convention; for those, an unresolved pair says only that the two summaries are not separated under a $t$-type reading at the stated count, and whether that is thin evidence or a test mismatched to a skewed quantity is a property of both, which Section~\ref{sec:tests} estimates on the papers where it can be seen. The bold cell is the extreme of the $k$ printed arms and its rival the second extreme; a two-sample test on that pair, read as if the pair had been fixed in advance, is anti-conservative under the null of equal arms, so the pass rate is over-generous, and Holm within a table does not correct that. Holm's correction \cite{holm1979simple} within a table is reported beside the raw rate; it controls the family-wise error over the table's bold-cell claims, the family this audit tests, which is smaller than the family of all pairwise comparisons the authors' own correction would face, so it understates that correction.

\paragraph{The format hazard, and the two instruments} Metaheuristics tables often print the mean and the standard deviation as two rows or two columns rather than as $a \pm b$ in one cell; the parser, frozen before this corpus was chosen, reads such a mean as bar-less. This was stated in the plan, and the image-based reader census of Section~\ref{sec:census} is the instrument this paper quotes; its protocol gains one value of one field for the separate-row layout. The parser and its rules have not been changed since the companion audits; this study adds readings on its output; the parser's own numbers on this corpus, which measure its unit more than the field (a third of its comparisons come from all-bold rows and reference lists, and most of its tested ones from a differential-evolution phrase read as a run count), are in the Supplement, Section~S1, and none is quoted as a rate of the field.

\section{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

The plan fixed, before any download, a corpus of fully open-access CC-BY journals with a metaheuristics output, selected by an API query so that the corpus is reproducible: every article of 2024 or 2025 whose title or abstract matches a phrase list (metaheuristic, evolutionary algorithm, genetic algorithm, particle swarm, differential evolution, swarm intelligence, ant colony, simulated annealing, nature-inspired, bio-inspired optimization, grey wolf, whale optimization, memetic, NSGA) in OpenAlex.\footnote{\url{https://openalex.org}; the query and the list are in the released code.} Hybrid journals, this one included, were excluded because their open-access subset is a selected sample. The query listed \nOListed{} papers across \nOJournalsListed{} journals. Three publishers refused automated download of their PDFs. A released record of throttled requests, five random listed papers per journal at five-second intervals, gives the answers: the four MDPI journals returned no PDF to any of \nFaMdpiAttempts{} requests (status 403 or no answer; \nFaMdpiListed{} listed papers), \emph{PeerJ Computer Science} 403 behind a bot screen to all \nFaPeerjComputerScienceAttempts{}, and \emph{Complex \& Intelligent Systems} status 200 with an HTML challenge page in place of the file in \nFaComplexAndIntelligentSystemsHtmlOnly{} of \nFaComplexAndIntelligentSystemsAttempts{}; \emph{Scientific Reports} returned the PDF for \nFaScientificReportsPdfOk{} of \nFaScientificReportsAttempts{}. Europe PMC mirrors two of the journals; its search API returned the identifiers, its full-text endpoint serves the article XML, and its PDF endpoint and NCBI's answered a bot challenge (the attempts are in the same record). After review a sanctioned bulk route was found and used: PMC's Open Access Subset on AWS, a public bucket served over plain HTTPS with the PMCID from NCBI's identifier converter, holds the PDFs, and \nPmFetchOk{} of the \nPmFetchListed{} listed papers of \emph{Biomimetics} and \emph{PeerJ Computer Science} were fetched that way (\nPmFetchNoPmcid{} have no PMCID). They are not part of the corpus, whose census, full-text reading and kits were drawn before; the text scan, the kind classifier and the frozen parser were run on them unchanged, and Table~\ref{tab:pmc} reports them as a labelled contrast, added after the data. The corpus is therefore the \emph{Scientific Reports} subset: \nOListedSrep{} papers (\nOListedSrepTwentyFour{} of 2024 and \nOListedSrepTwentyFive{} of 2025), \nOFetched{} fetched and \nOParsed{} parsed. The counts used below are these: \nOListedSrep{} listed and fetched and scanned, \nOParsed{} parsed by the frozen parser, \nCzPapersEligible{} with a parser-detected table caption (the census base; the \nCzExcludedPapers{} outside it, \nCzExcludedApplication{} of them applications and \nCzExcludedNewOptimizer{} new-optimizer papers, have no table the parser could find, and \nCzExcludedNotParsed{} were not parsed), \nKafPapers{} labelled by both labellers. This is a deviation from the plan: the corpus is one megajournal's metaheuristics output rather than seven journals', and no number in this paper describes the venues in which this journal's readers publish.

\paragraph{Two literatures} A language model read the title and abstract of each listed paper and assigned one kind: \emph{new optimizer} (a new, modified or hybrid metaheuristic evaluated against others), \emph{comparison} (existing optimizers benchmarked, none proposed), \emph{application} (one or more metaheuristics applied to a domain problem, the application being the contribution), \emph{survey or theory}, or \emph{other}; the prompt asked also whether the optimizer is metaphor-named and whether the abstract mentions benchmark functions. The prompt, reproduced in the Supplement (Section~S4), sees the title and abstract only and names no reporting feature; its definition of a new optimizer, one evaluated against others, does select the papers that benchmark, which is where the convention applies, and the rates below, computed from the full text the classifier never saw, measure how far those papers follow it. By this reading the corpus is \nKcApplication{} application papers, \nKcNewOptimizer{} new-optimizer papers, \nKcComparison{} comparisons, \nKcSurveyTheory{} surveys and \nKcOther{} others. The ratio is a property of matching the phrase list against a general-science journal's abstracts, not of the field, and the two kinds report differently enough that every rate below is given by kind. A second vendor's model labelled a random \nKaPapers{} papers under the same prompt and agreed with the first on \nKaAgree{} (\nKaPctAgree{}, Wilson interval \cite{wilson1927probable} \nKaCiAgree{}; Cohen's $\kappa$ \cite{cohen1960coefficient} \nKaKappa{}). The disagreements run one way: the second labeller files \nKaDirSecondNewOptimizer{} of the hundred as new-optimizer papers against the first's \nKaDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, moving \nKaDirApplicationToNewOptimizer{} papers from application to new optimizer and \nKaDirNewOptimizerToApplication{} the other way, so the first labeller's new-optimizer stratum has precision \nKaDirPrecisionVsSecond{} and recall \nKaDirRecallVsSecond{} against the second's on the hundred (\nKafDirPrecisionVsSecond{} and \nKafDirRecallVsSecond{} over the whole corpus), and under the second labeller the stratum would be roughly a third larger. The \nKafStDisputedToNewOptimizerPapers{} papers the second labeller moves from application to new optimizer state a run count in \nKafStDisputedToNewOptimizerRuns{} and name a test in \nKafStDisputedToNewOptimizerTest{}, between the two strata, which is where the boundary lies. The second labeller was then run over the whole corpus (\nKafPapers{} papers both labelled): the two agree on \nKafPctAgree{} (Cohen's $\kappa$ \nKafKappa{}), the second files \nKafDirSecondNewOptimizer{} papers as new-optimizer against the first's \nKafDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, and Table~\ref{tab:kinds} gives the scan rates under the second labelling and on the \nKafStBothNewOptimizerPapers{} and \nKafStBothApplicationPapers{} papers both labellers agree on; neither labelling is adjudicated by a person. The direction of the reporting gap between kinds does not depend on which labeller draws the line; its size does (\nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} and \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} under the first, \nKafStSecondNewOptimizerRuns{} and \nKafStSecondNewOptimizerTest{} under the second): on the double-labelled hundred, the papers both call new optimizers state a run count in \nKaStBothNewOptimizerRuns{} of cases and name a test in \nKaStBothNewOptimizerTest{}, the papers either calls one in \nKaStEitherNewOptimizerRuns{} and \nKaStEitherNewOptimizerTest{}, the papers both call applications in \nKaStBothApplicationRuns{} and \nKaStBothApplicationTest{}, and the \nKaStDisputedPapers{} papers the two labellers file differently in \nKaStDisputedRuns{} and \nKaStDisputedTest{}, between the two strata. On the metaphor question the two agreed on \nKaMetaphorAgree{} of \nKaMetaphorPapers{}, \nKaMetaphorNoneDisagreements{} of the disagreements being ``no'' against an undefined ``none''; the question is not used further.

\paragraph{Two readings of the text} A regular-expression scan of the full text, released with the paper, records for every paper whether it states a number of independent runs, which statistical tests it names (Wilcoxon, Friedman, $t$, Mann--Whitney, Kruskal--Wallis, ANOVA, a $p$-value, a Bonferroni or Holm correction, the $+/=/-$ convention), which benchmark suite it names (a CEC year, BBOB, the classical functions), whether it cites the methodology papers of Section~\ref{sec:related} by title, and whether it carries the journal's production note ``Significant values are in bold''. The scan is a strict-match count: it finds the phrasings it was written for and no others. A language model then read the full text of \nTrAllPapers{} papers (Section~\ref{sec:tests}); the abstract and Table~\ref{tab:kinds} quote the scan because it covers every paper, and the model's reading, which covers the new-optimizer stratum and a random hundred, is printed beside it. Table~\ref{tab:kinds} gives both by kind.

\paragraph{The plan, and what was decided after the data} The plan (released with the code) was committed to the author's repository within the minute the bulk fetch of the corpus began, after two forty-paper probes of arXiv and ten probe PDFs per journal; it is an analysis plan, not a registration with an independent registry. It fixed the unit, the test (Welch's $t$, two-sided, at the stated count, an unlabelled bar read as a deviation, $p \geq 0.05$ unresolved), the corpus rule and its seven journals, the five-hundred-paper census under the frozen protocol, the four instruments and the four questions, the citing contrast among them. Decided after the data, and marked as such where they appear: the classifier's prompt and the scan's patterns (written during the fetch), the whole-text reading, the Mean/Std-row re-read instruction and the table-by-table merge rule, the means-only filter, the bold-rule sentence rescue, the all-bold-row and back-matter readings of the parser, the journal-note stratum, the genuine-holder restriction, the verdict cross-tab protocol, the shape and orientation strata, the two kits and the arXiv corpus. The review rounds that shaped these were run by models, four of them by the census reader's own model. Three of the plan's statements were not kept as written: the corpus is one journal of seven; the human labels it promised for the strata the reviewers of a companion audit had asked for were not produced for this submission (a labelling kit is released, Section~\ref{sec:validation}); and the benchmark-comparison stratum of its third question holds \nKcComparison{} papers and no comparison, so that question is not answered.

\subsection{The reader census}
\label{sec:census}

\paragraph{Protocol} An image-based reader (Gemini 3.1 Pro, relay model id \texttt{gemini/}\allowbreak\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview}, served through an OpenAI-compatible relay; every model reading in this paper names its model and command in the released run records) was given every page of \nCzPapersDrawn{} randomly drawn papers on which the parser had detected a table caption, \nCzPagesDrawn{} pages, rendered at twice print size with nothing marked, together with the paper's run sentences, under the protocol released with the companion audits: list every results table on the page; for each, the orientation, whether any cell prints a dispersion, what the dispersion is (SD, SE, CI, unlabelled), its layout (in the cell, in parentheses, in a separate column, in a separate row, none), the run count and where it was found; then every group with exactly one bold cell, up to forty per table (\nCrcTablesAtGroupCap{} tables reach the cap), with the bold cell's mean and dispersion and the best non-bold cell's mean and dispersion, values as printed. The one change to the protocol for this corpus is the \emph{separate row} value of the layout field. The run sentences are supplied by the text scan, so a run count ``found in the paper'' is one the scan's patterns found and the reader accepted, applied to every table of that paper; the reading whose count comes from the caption or the page alone is given below. The reader's groups are scored under the parser's unit, a group whose bold cell is not the better value in its stated direction, or ties its rival, being dropped as the parser drops it, and Welch's test is computed on the reader's values exactly as on the parser's. \nCzPagesRead{} of the \nCzPagesDrawn{} pages were read; the rest timed out at every attempt.

\paragraph{The first reading} Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the census in two columns. Under the first protocol the reader lists \nCrResultsTables{} results tables, of which \nCrTablesAnyBar{} print any dispersion, and \nCzGroups{} single-bold groups in \nCzPapersWithGroups{} papers: \nCrAllNeither{} \nCrAllNeitherCi{} print no dispersion on either side, \nCrAllOneSide{} on one side, and \nCrAllTestable{} \nCrAllTestableCi{} are testable, both dispersions on the page and a run count found. Of the \nCrAllTested{} tested groups (\nCrAllTestableN{} testable, less \nCrAllZeroBoth{} whose two deviations are both printed as zero, on which no test is defined, and \nCrAllUnreadable{} whose values the reader could not make out), held by \nCrPpPapers{} papers, \nCrAllFail{} \nCrAllFailCi{} are not separable from their rival by Welch's $t$ at the stated run count.

\paragraph{What the reader gets wrong, and the re-read} This reading has, on one layout, the blind spot the parser was set aside for. When a table prints the mean and the standard deviation as two rows (a \emph{Mean} row and a \emph{Std} row per function), the reader files the table as having no dispersion, lists the mean rows as bar-less groups and the \emph{Std} rows as further bar-less groups: it gave the \emph{separate row} value to \nCrFormSeparateRow{} of \nCrResultsTables{} results tables while the full-text reading files \nTrNewOptimizerLayoutSeparateRowPct{} of the new-optimizer papers as printing their deviation that way, and its own notes say so on the affected pages. It also lists rows labelled \emph{Std}, \emph{Rank} or \emph{Time}, which are not the objective's statistic over runs, and rows labelled \emph{Best}, \emph{Worst}, \emph{Median}, \emph{Min} or \emph{Max} as the whole label, which are statistics over runs that owe no deviation (a ``Max error'' row is a mean and is kept), as groups when such a row happens to carry one bold cell: \nCrRowsStdRowGroups{} and \nCrRowsBestRowGroups{} groups, plus \nCrRowsNotMeansTableGroups{} groups in tables whose caption names ranks, test verdicts or settings; \nCrRowsOnlyNeither{} of the labelled rows are bar-less and \nCrRowsOnlyTested{} of them were tested. The rates over the groups that are means over runs are given as the \emph{means} rows of Table~\ref{tab:headline}.

The Mean/Std-row blind spot was repaired after the census, and the repair is reported beside it, not in place of it. Every census page holding a results table filed as having no dispersion and at least one single-bold group, \nCrrrPagesInKits{} pages in two draws (the first from the papers the full-text reading files as Mean/Std rows plus the pages whose reader notes mention a separate row, the second every other such page), was read again under the same protocol with one added instruction: to take the \emph{Std} cell beneath or beside a mean as that mean's dispersion and never to list a summary row as a group. \nCrrrPagesReread{} pages came back (\nCrrrPagesNewOptimizer{} of new-optimizer papers, \nCrrrPagesApplication{} of application papers). The re-read ran at an output budget of eight thousand tokens against the census's twelve thousand, and \nCrrrPagesTruncatedFirstPass{} of its pages were cut at that budget, which leaves the page-level fields empty in the record; those pages were read a third time at the census's budget (a third kit, released), after which \nCrrrPagesTruncated{} are still cut, and a re-read cut at the budget keeps its original. The re-read is merged table by table: a re-read table replaces its original unless it is such a truncation and lists fewer groups, and a complete re-read that lists fewer groups is the reader's correction under the instruction (a whole bold row, a table of deviations, two bold cells per row) and is accepted: \nCrrrTablesReplaced{} of \nCrrrTablesReread{} re-read tables replaced their original, \nCrrrCorrectionsAccepted{} of them with fewer groups (\nCrrrCorrectionsGroupsOld{} in the originals, \nCrrrCorrectionsGroupsNew{} after), and \nCrrrTablesKept{} truncated re-reads were refused (\nCrrrKeptGroupsOld{} groups in their originals against \nCrrrKeptGroupsNew{} returned). The rule was written after the census and revised after review; the first version refused every re-read with fewer groups. On the re-read pages the reader had listed \nCrrrBeforeGroups{} groups with both dispersions on \nCrrrBeforeBarred{}; after the merge there are \nCrrrAfterGroups{} groups with both dispersions on \nCrrrAfterBarred{}, \nCrrrTablesSeparateRowAfter{} results tables are filed as separate-row and \nCrrrTablesNoneAfter{} results tables are still filed as having no dispersion, so the repair recovered part of the layout and not all of it. As a sensitivity reading, taken wholesale, truncations included, the census would read \nCrwAllNeither{} bar-less, \nCrwAllTestable{} testable and \nCrwAllFail{} of \nCrwAllTested{} tested groups unresolved, and \nCrwMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} \nCrwMeansNewOptimizerNeitherCi{} of the new-optimizer means bar-less against \nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} under the rule. Where the residual sits, reader miss or table without a deviation, is visible against the full-text reading (a per-group answer waits on the released kit): in the papers it files as printing the deviation in a separate row, \nCrcRlSeparateRowBarless{} of the \nCrcRlSeparateRowGroups{} mean groups (\nCrcRlSeparateRowPapers{} papers) are still bar-less after the re-read, in the papers filed as a separate column \nCrcRlSeparateColumnBarless{} of \nCrcRlSeparateColumnGroups{} (\nCrcRlSeparateColumnPapers{} papers), in the papers filed as printing it in the cell \nCrcRlSameCellBarless{}, and in the papers the full-text reading did not cover, mostly application papers, \nCrcRlUnreadBarless{} of \nCrcRlUnreadGroups{}. Merged over the census the re-read reading is \nCrcAllGroups{} groups in \nCrcAllPapers{} papers, \nCrcAllNeither{} \nCrcAllNeitherCi{} bar-less, \nCrcAllTestable{} \nCrcAllTestableCi{} testable, and \nCrcAllFail{} \nCrcAllFailCi{} of \nCrcAllTested{} tested groups in \nCrcAllTestedPapers{} papers not separable; over the groups that are means over runs, \nCrcMeansNeither{} \nCrcMeansNeitherCi{} bar-less, \nCrcMeansTestable{} testable and \nCrcMeansFail{} \nCrcMeansFailCi{} of \nCrcMeansTested{} tested groups unresolved, the number the abstract quotes (\nCrcPpmPapers{} papers, Kish ratio \nCrcPpmEffectivePapers{}, median paper \nCrcPpmMedianRate{}, \nCrcPpmWithoutTopThreeFail{} on \nCrcPpmWithoutTopThreeTested{} without the three multi-objective papers). The re-read reading is the one the abstract quotes; the instruction was written after the census and both readings are released. Under the second labeller's kinds the bold means print no deviation in \nCrcKsecMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} of the new-optimizer and \nCrcKsecMeansApplicationNeither{} of the application groups, and on the papers both labellers agree on \nCrcKbothMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} and \nCrcKbothMeansApplicationNeither{}. The re-read census still contains the layouts the reader reads badly, and Section~\ref{sec:validation} says what was prepared to measure that.

\paragraph{Where the tested groups sit} The re-read census's tested groups are concentrated: four papers hold \nCrcPpTopFourShare{} of them and ten hold \nCrcPpTopTenShare{}; the largest holds \nCrcPpLargestPaperTested{} and leaves \nCrcPpLargestPaperFail{} of them unresolved. The three largest are multi-objective studies, indicator tables (IGD, hypervolume) with rank-sum verdicts of the PlatEMO kind, and not the single-objective mean-and-deviation convention the introduction describes: they hold \nCrcPpTopThreeShare{} of the tested groups and leave \nCrcPpTopThreeOnlyFail{} of them unresolved, the other \nCrcPpWithoutTopThreeTestedPapers{} papers \nCrcPpWithoutTopThreeFail{} \nCrcPpWithoutTopThreeFailCi{} on \nCrcPpWithoutTopThreeTested{}; over every paper whose OpenAlex title or abstract matches a multi-objective pattern (\nCrcMoPapersTested{} tested papers, a split made after the data) the rate is \nCrcMoFail{} on \nCrcMoTested{} against \nCrcSoFail{} \nCrcSoFailCi{} on \nCrcSoTested{} single-objective groups; as a descriptive measure of that concentration, the Kish ratio of the squared sum to the sum of squares of the papers' group counts is \nCrcPpEffectivePapers{}; the intra-paper correlation of the unresolved indicator is \nCrcPpIcc{} (itself estimated from \nCrcPpPapers{} clusters), and the design effect that belongs to a share weighted by group counts, the cluster-robust variance over the binomial one, is \nCrcPpDesignEffect{} (Kish's $1 + (n^{*} - 1)\rho$ with $n^{*}$ the size-weighted cluster size gives \nCrcPpDesignEffectKish{}), so the \nCrcAllTested{} tested groups carry about as much information as \nCrcPpEffectiveGroups{} independent ones; the printed intervals already reflect this. Across the \nCrcPpPapers{} papers the per-paper unresolved share has median \nCrcPpMedianRate{} (quartiles \nCrcPpQORate{} and \nCrcPpQTRate{}) and mean \nCrcPpMacroRate{} \nCrcPpMacroRateCi{}, the interval a bootstrap over papers as units, so that each paper weighs the same; \nCrcPpPapersAnyFail{} papers have at least one unresolved group, the rate without the largest paper is \nCrcPpWithoutLargestFail{} \nCrcPpWithoutLargestFailCi{} on \nCrcPpWithoutLargestTested{}, and without the four largest \nCrcPpWithoutTopFourFail{} \nCrcPpWithoutTopFourFailCi{} on \nCrcPpWithoutTopFourTested{}. Every interval on a group rate in this paper is a percentile cluster bootstrap over papers; with this few papers, and cluster sizes this unequal, it is an approximation that under-covers: taking the \nCrcPpPapers{} papers, with their group counts and unresolved shares, as the population and simulating the census \nCrcPpBootCoverageSims{} times, the nominal 95 percent interval covers the population rate in \nCrcPpBootCoverage{} of the simulations (Monte Carlo \nCrcPpBootCoverageMcCi{}), and a cluster-robust Wald interval on \nCrcPpPapers{} less one degrees of freedom, \nCrcPpWaldCi{} on the census, covers it in \nCrcPpWaldCoverage{} of the same simulations; both are short of nominal by about the same amount; the same simulation, at the same settings, on each of the \nCrcCovStrata{} strata for which an unresolved-rate interval is printed gives coverage from \nCrcCovMin{} (\nCrcCovMinStratum{}) to \nCrcCovMax{} (\nCrcCovMaxStratum{}), and \nCrcCovMeansOnly{} on the means-only stratum the abstract quotes; the per-paper quartiles are printed beside it so that the reader can see what it resamples.

\paragraph{What the summaries look like} A deviation at least as large as its mean is common in benchmark tables, where the error on a solved function is a bounded, strongly right-skewed quantity near zero; on such a quantity the summary announces a shape on which a $t$-type check is poorly calibrated and the guides recommend a rank test. This stratification was made after the data. In the re-read census \nCrcShPctTestedSdGeMean{} of the tested groups have a deviation at least as large as the mean on one arm (\nCrcShSdGeMeanBoldOnlyTested{} on the bold arm only, \nCrcShSdGeMeanBoldOnlyFail{} unresolved; \nCrcShSdGeMeanRivalOnlyTested{} on the rival only, \nCrcShSdGeMeanRivalOnlyFail{}; \nCrcShSdGeMeanBothTested{} on both, \nCrcShSdGeMeanBothFail{}; \nCrcShBoldZeroPmZero{} bold cells are printed as $0 \pm 0$) and \nCrcShPctTestedZeroSd{} a printed deviation of exactly zero on one arm; the \nCrcShZeroBothGroups{} groups with both deviations printed as zero (\nCrcShZeroBothPapers{} papers) are the most separable of all and are counted, not tested, which is the direction unfavourable to the field: counted as passes they give \nCrcShZeroBothAsPassPctFail{} on \nCrcShZeroBothAsPassTested{}. The unresolved rate is \nCrcShSdGeMeanFail{} on the \nCrcShSdGeMeanTested{} groups with a deviation at least the mean on either arm and \nCrcShSdLtMeanFail{} \nCrcShSdLtMeanFailCi{} on the \nCrcShSdLtMeanTested{} with the deviation below the mean on both. A deviation at least the mean imposes no ceiling on $t$ by itself; with both arms so shaped and the quantity non-negative, $|t|$ cannot exceed $\sqrt{n}\,|m_2 - m_1| / \sqrt{m_1^2 + m_2^2}$, and that ceiling falls below the critical value in \nCrcShCeilingBinds{} tested groups (\nCrcShCeilingBindsPctTested{}), all of them unresolved; the rest of the stratum's excess of unresolved pairs is the shape, not a cap. The $p$-values themselves are not near the threshold: \nCrcPdFailPAtLeastHalf{} of the unresolved pairs have $p \geq 0.5$ and \nCrcPdFailPBelowTenth{} have $p$ below one tenth, \nCrcPdPassPBelowThousandth{} of the passes have $p$ below one thousandth, and the rate is \nCrcPdPctFailAtOnePercent{} at the one percent level and \nCrcPdPctFailAtTenPercent{} at ten percent. \nCrcPdBothMeansBelowFloor{} tested pairs have both means below $10^{-10}$, where a verdict either way is vacuous; without them the rate is \nCrcPdWithoutFloorFail{}. On the stratum where a $t$-type check is least objectionable, means with the deviation below the mean on both arms, in single-objective papers, at twenty runs or more and not in a whole-row table, the rate is \nCrcLeastFail{} \nCrcLeastFailCi{} on \nCrcLeastTested{} groups in \nCrcLeastTestedPapers{} papers.

The question the protocol did not ask was then asked, after review, of every column-oriented table: does every bold cell of the table lie in one row, or in one column, and under which label? The same model, on the same page images, says yes for \nCrcWholeRowTables{} of the \nCrcColTables{} tables, a proposed method's row or a header row of numbers, and no for \nCrcWholeRowTablesNo{} (\nCrcWholeRowTablesUnanswered{} unanswered; a table with fewer than three bold cells is counted as no, since two bold cells in one row are also the proposed method winning both columns). The whole-row tables hold \nCrcWrGroups{} groups in \nCrcWrPapers{} papers, \nCrcWrNeither{} of them bar-less and only \nCrcWrTested{} tested, so they weigh on the bar-less rates and not on the unresolved rate: without them the census reads \nCrcNowrFail{} \nCrcNowrFailCi{} on \nCrcNowrTested{} tested groups, and over the means \nCrcNowrMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} of the new-optimizer and \nCrcNowrMeansApplicationNeither{} of the application groups print no deviation, against \nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} and \nCrcMeansApplicationNeither{} with them; what a whole bold row claims is not the best mean in a column, and those groups are a typographic convention the census now names rather than a reporting breach. \subsection{By kind of paper}
\label{sec:kinds}

Table~\ref{tab:kinds} splits everything by kind. The new-optimizer papers are where the convention lives: \nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} state a run count by the scan and \nTrNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} by the model reading, with thirty the count in \nSkNewOptimizerPctThirtyModal{} and \nTrNewOptimizerPctRunsThirty{} respectively; \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} name a statistical test by the scan (Wilcoxon in \nSkNewOptimizerWilcoxon{} papers, Friedman in \nSkNewOptimizerFriedman{}) and \nTrNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} by the model; \nSkNewOptimizerPctCec{} name a CEC suite; \nSkNewOptimizerPctCitesMethodology{} cite one of the methodology guides. The application papers, \nKcApplication{} of them, state a run count in \nSkApplicationPctRunsStated{} of cases and a test in \nSkApplicationPctTestNamed{}, and the test they name most often is the $t$-test (\nSkApplicationTTest{} papers) rather than the Wilcoxon (\nSkApplicationWilcoxon{}). \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The convention is on the page, in a column the cell-reader cannot see} The parser's frozen headline for this corpus (Supplement, Section~S1) measures something narrower than a reporting rate: how often these papers print the deviation \emph{inside the cell}, which is the machine-learning habit and not this one. The reader, which sees the \emph{Std} column or row once told to, finds no dispersion on either side for \nCrcAllNeither{} of its groups and for \nCrcNewOptimizerNeither{} of those in new-optimizer papers. Two lessons follow. For an audit, an instrument frozen on one literature measures a different thing on another until its blind spots are named in advance and measured afterwards; here the hazard named in advance, the \emph{Std} column, was real, and two hazards not named, a differential-evolution term of art read as a run count and reference lists read as tables, were found by the reference set and by review. For an author, the convention as practised is checkable: where the deviation and the run count are printed, a reader can recompute the comparison, and the recomputation does not separate the bold cell from its rival at the stated run count in \nCrcAllFail{} of the re-read census's tested groups, in \nCrcAllTestedPapers{} papers, the per-paper rate varying from \nCrcPpQORate{} to \nCrcPpQTRate{} across the middle half of the re-read census's papers.

\paragraph{Two literatures under one name} \nKcApplication{} of the \nSkAllPapers{} papers this query returns are applications, and by the scan they state a run count in \nSkApplicationPctRunsStated{} of cases and name a test in \nSkApplicationPctTestNamed{}, against \nSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} and \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} for the papers that propose an optimizer; under the re-read census their groups are bar-less \nCrcApplicationNeither{} of the time against \nCrcNewOptimizerNeither{}. The gap survives the second labeller's line between the kinds (Section~\ref{sec:corpus}), holds on the \nPmScanned{} papers of the two journals fetched after review (Table~\ref{tab:pmc}: \nPmSkNewOptimizerPctRunsStated{} and \nPmSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} against \nPmSkApplicationPctRunsStated{} and \nPmSkApplicationPctTestNamed{}) and survives restriction to the application papers that hold a highlighted comparison (\nHkApplicationPctRunsStated{} and \nHkApplicationPctTestNamed{}). The guides are addressed to the second literature, and its run counts and test names mostly follow them; its deviations beside the bold cell and its bold legends do not (\nCrcMeansNewOptimizerNeither{} of its bold means have no deviation on their table, in \nCrcPbNewOptimizerPapersWithHits{} of \nCrcPbNewOptimizerPapers{} papers, while \nTrNewOptimizerLayoutNonePct{} of its papers print none anywhere; \nTrNewOptimizerBoldNotStatedPct{} never say what bold means). The first, where a metaheuristic meets a scheduling problem or a network's hyperparameters, states no run count in most cases and prints the best value bold. Reviewers of application papers have the same guides available.

\paragraph{What bold means} The model's reading finds a stated rule for bold in \nTrAllBoldBestPct{} of the papers it read and none in \nTrAllBoldNotStatedPct{}; the rule tying bold to a test is, in \nTrNoteFiledStatisticallyBest{} of the \nTrNotePapersRead{} read papers that carry it, the journal's production note ``Significant values are in bold'', present under the tables of \nBnAllWithNote{} papers, \nBnAllNoteNoTestNamed{} of which name no test, so that a legend inserted by production settles nothing. Meanwhile the parser finds a whole row in bold in \nRbRowBoldPapers{} papers: in \nRbNonHeaderRowBoldPapers{} of them a row other than the header, usually the proposed method's row, which is a typographic claim of a different kind, and in the rest only the header row the journal's house style sets in bold. When a whole row is bold every column holds exactly one bold cell, so both instruments read such a table as column groups won by that row; the parser's \nRbRowBoldOnlyN{} such comparisons are removed in one reading of the Supplement (Section~S1), and the census, asked that question table by table after review, finds \nCrcWholeRowTables{} whole-row and \nCrcWholeColTables{} whole-column tables holding \nCrcWrGroups{} and \nCrcWcGroups{} groups, nearly all of them bar-less, and is printed with and without them in Section~\ref{sec:census}. The guides already say what would settle it \cite{derrac2011practical,latorre2021prescription}, and the specialist venues' papers already do it: a legend written by the authors, ``bold: best mean over $N$ runs'', with the verdict of the named test marked on each competitor's cell. This paper recommends nothing new to authors; it measures how far that practice reaches this journal's new-optimizer papers, \nTrNewOptimizerPctMarkers{} by the first reader and more by the second (Section~\ref{sec:tests}), and almost none of its applications. The one recommendation aimed elsewhere is to the publisher: a production style that inserts ``Significant values are in bold'' under the tables of \nBnAllNoteNoTestNamed{} papers that name no test would serve its readers better by requiring an author-written legend.

\paragraph{What an unresolved cell does not say} A bold cell that Welch's $t$ on the summaries cannot separate from its rival is not a refuted algorithm, and the rate of such cells is neither an error rate nor a bound: the pair is the extreme of the printed arms, and a $t$-type reading is poorly calibrated on the skewed quantities of a fifth of the tested groups. The authors' claim, by the full-text reading, is a rank test on the per-run values of each function in most papers and an aggregate over the suite in a minority, and an unresolved pair under a $t$-type reading of two summaries leaves either claim where it was; what is unresolved is the typographic claim that one printed number is the best, at the run count the authors state, on the evidence the authors chose to print. The census rate is a rate of bold cells whose printed summaries are not separated under a $t$-type reading, read with the concentration of Section~\ref{sec:census} in mind; it is \nCrcShSdLtMeanFail{} on the cells whose deviation is below the mean on both arms and \nCrcLeastFail{} on the stratum where such a reading is least objectionable.

\paragraph{The test the field names and the test the page supports} \nSkNewOptimizerPctTestNamed{} of the new-optimizer papers name a statistical test, Wilcoxon and Friedman most often, as the guides ask. The page, however, prints means and deviations, which admit a $t$-type comparison and not a rank test; the Wilcoxon verdict, computed on per-run values the paper does not print, reaches the reader only as a symbol or a $p$-value where the paper prints one. That is why the audit uses Welch's $t$ on the summaries: not because it is the right test for the data, but because it is the standard per-cell test the printed summaries admit, and a reader who wants to check a bold cell is in exactly that position. The printed table does admit the guides' aggregate test, a Wilcoxon signed-rank paired across functions on the per-function summaries, which \nTrNewOptimizerOnPerFunctionMeans{} of the new-optimizer papers read report using; that test asks whether the optimizer wins over the suite, not whether one cell's bold is supported, and this audit does not recompute it. On the skewed, bounded errors that led the guides to recommend rank tests a $t$-type check on the summaries and a rank test on the runs can disagree in either direction, and for \nCrcShPctTestedSdGeMean{} of the tested groups the summary itself, a deviation at least the size of a non-negative mean, announces that shape. On the pairs the census records, the bold cell and its best rival, every difference has the bold cell's sign, so a signed-rank across them would be fixed by the row count; that is the other reason the aggregate test is not recomputed here. The census rate is a statement about the bold cell, a typographic claim, and estimates what the printed summaries support for it by a $t$-type test, not what the authors' own test would find; where both verdicts are on the page (Section~\ref{sec:tests}) they agree in \nVcPctAgree{} of cases and the disagreements run towards Welch leaving unresolved what the paper's rank test reports significance. Authors in this journal could close this gap in either direction, by printing the test's verdict beside every bold cell, or by releasing the per-run values so that the named test can be recomputed; the guides recommend both.

\paragraph{Limitations} The corpus is what one general megajournal published on metaheuristics in 2024 and 2025, \nOListedSrep{} of the \nOListed{} papers the plan's query listed across \nOJournalsListed{} journals. The others were not fetched: three publishers' PDF servers answered the throttled requests with a refusal or a challenge page, and for the two journals Europe PMC mirrors (\nFaMirroredListed{} listed papers) the PDF endpoints first tried answered a bot challenge, while PMC's bulk route, found after review, served \nPmFetchOk{} of their PDFs; those papers carry the scan, the classifier and the parser (Table~\ref{tab:pmc}) and not the census, the full-text reading or the kits, so the census is one journal's and the one-journal corpus was in part a choice of fetch route; no number here describes the venues in which this journal's readers publish, and a general-science megajournal's review may hold the convention less firmly than a specialist venue. The abstract's model-reading rates for the new-optimizer stratum are the first labeller's; the second labeller's \nKafStDisputedToNewOptimizerPapers{} added papers were scanned and not read by the model. Every stratum is by a title-and-abstract label: over the whole corpus the two labellers agree on \nKafPctAgree{} of papers, the second files \nKafDirSecondNewOptimizer{} as new-optimizer against the first's \nKafDirFirstNewOptimizer{}, Table~\ref{tab:kinds} and Section~\ref{sec:census} give the rates under both and on the papers both agree on, and neither labelling has been adjudicated by a person, so ``new-optimizer'' and ``application'' in this paper mean labelled so by the model named; the classifier's definition of a new optimizer, one evaluated against others, selects the papers that benchmark, and the full-text reading of Table~\ref{tab:kinds} covers the first labeller's stratum only. One model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) produced every reading in this paper other than the second-labeller checks: the classifier and the census, the re-read that followed it, the verdict reader and the full-text reader. The reference set measures the reader against the parser's cells, in-cell layouts only; on the separate-column and separate-row layouts, which hold \nCrcBarredGroupsSeparateLayoutPct{} of the reader's barred groups, its pairing of means with deviations is checked against a second vendor's model on two hundred kit items and against the impossible-pair check, not against a person, and the kits are released for one; the second model was handed the reader's caption and group label, direction included, so its agreement is a check of the values and not an independent draw. The re-read was the same model under a second instruction, merged table by table under a rule that keeps the original where the re-read was cut at its output budget and accepts a complete re-read otherwise; the rule was revised after review, both readings are printed, and the repair reached the new-optimizer stratum more than the application stratum. Nothing checks the full-text fields against a person; the per-cell-verdict field is the one on which the two readers disagree most, every disagreement a verdict the second reader quotes and the first misses, and the \nTaMarkersNewOptimizerFirstNoSecondYes{} disputed new-optimizer papers are listed in the release for adjudication. The census protocol files a table with one whole bold row as column-oriented; the question was asked afterwards of every such table, and of every row-oriented table with three or more single-bold groups for the whole-column case, by the same model; \nCrcWholeRowTables{} and \nCrcWholeColTables{} tables answer yes, and the rates are printed without them. The guides' aggregate test, a Wilcoxon signed-rank paired across functions, is computable from a printed table with enough function rows; the census records only the bold cell and its best rival per function, so it is not recomputed here and the per-cell rate does not test it. On the \nVcPapersLocated{} papers where a printed verdict could be located, \nVcWelchFailPrintedSignificant{} of the \nVcWelchFailLocated{} unresolved cells coincide with a significant printed rank verdict; that share is the only estimate here of how much of the unresolved rate belongs to the test, and it is not extrapolated. The census's tested groups are concentrated in \nCrcPpPapers{} papers, three multi-objective studies holding \nCrcPpTopThreeShare{} (Kish ratio \nCrcPpEffectivePapers{}, design effect \nCrcPpDesignEffect{}); the percentile cluster bootstrap is an approximation on so few clusters (simulated coverage \nCrcPpBootCoverage{} for a nominal 95 percent, and \nCrcPpWaldCoverage{} for a cluster-robust Wald interval), intervals are withheld where the effective count is below eight, the simulated coverage of the printed unresolved-rate intervals runs from \nCrcCovMin{} to \nCrcCovMax{}, and the per-paper distribution and a paper-weighted rate are printed beside it; the intervals are sampling intervals over papers and do not include the spread across the labeller and merge choices printed in Section~\ref{sec:census}, which is narrow beside the bar-less interval and of the same order as the unresolved-rate one. Every measured class of reader error, the impossible pairs, the rival values the second model disputes, the run count borrowed from the paper's sentences, pushes the unresolved rate up, so the census rate is more likely over- than understated by the instrument; the one opposing force is the extreme-of-arms selection under the null. The verdict cross-tab rests on \nVcPapersLocated{} papers, three holding \nVcTopThreeShare{} of the pairs, and no interval is printed for it. The headline reading is a mixture: two-sided and unpaired at the reader's count, taken from the caption, the page or the paper's run sentences and applied to every table of the paper, push the unresolved rate up, an unlabelled bar read as a sample deviation pushes it down, the bold cell is the extreme of the printed arms so the pair-test is anti-conservative under the null, and Table~\ref{tab:headline} and the Supplement give the alternatives; the shape, orientation, note, bold-rule and citing strata were made after the data; none of the readings is a claim about what the authors would have found with their raw data. The parser's rates on this corpus measure its smallest-count rule and the format more than the evidence, and none of them is quoted as a rate of the field. The plan is an analysis plan committed to the author's repository as the fetch began, not a registration, and the review rounds that shaped the paper were run by models.

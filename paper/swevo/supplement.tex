\section{The frozen parser on this corpus}
\label{app:parser}

The parser, unchanged from the companion audits, detected \nATablesDetected{} table captions in the \nOParsed{} papers and read \nAComparisons{} highlighted comparisons in \nAClaimPapers{} of them; \nAPctNeitherCi{} print no dispersion in the cell, \nAPctTestable{} are testable, and \nAPctFailCi{} of the \nANTestable{} testable comparisons (\nAPapersTestable{} papers) fail Welch's test at the parser's run count. These are the numbers the plan fixed, and on this format they measure the instrument's unit more than the field, in three ways the reference set and review exposed: the deviation sits in a column or row the cell-level parser cannot read; this journal's house style sets header rows in bold and many papers set the proposed method's row in bold, so that \nRbRowBoldOnlyN{} comparisons (\nRbRowBoldPapers{} papers) come from all-bold rows and \nPrBackMatterAllComparisons{} from reference lists whose ``volume (issue)'' against ``page (year)'' parses as a mean with a deviation; and the smallest run count stated anywhere in the paper, the reading least favourable to separation on machine-learning papers, is here often not a run count, so that \nPrTestedAtFiveOrFewer{} of the testable comparisons, in \nPrTestedAtFiveOrFewerPapers{} papers, are tested at five runs or fewer, most from the phrase ``four trial vector generation strategies'' in two differential-evolution papers whose text states fifty-one and thirty runs. On the census pages the parser lists \nCzPipeGroups{} comparisons and the reader lists the parser's bold cell for \nCzPipeFoundPct{} of them. Table~\ref{tab:span} removes each artefact in turn; the cleaned reading, without the back-matter rows, the zero deviations and the interval cells and at the text scan's run count, is \nPrCleanedScanFail{} on \nPrCleanedScanN{} comparisons in \nPrCleanedScanPapers{} papers. None of the parser's subgroup contrasts is quoted in this paper, none of its rates is quoted as a rate of the field, and the census is the instrument to quote; the frozen headline is kept because the plan fixed it and because a filter on an instrument's output is easier to argue about than a change to the instrument.

Table~\ref{tab:span} gives the parser's testable set under each reading named above; the full set of readings of the companion audits, the arXiv cs.NE cross-check and the per-paper uncertainty census are in the release and not quoted here.

\section{Readings on the census}
\label{app:readings}

\paragraph{A check the run record allows} For a quantity bounded below by zero, an error to the optimum or a distance, the sample deviation over $n$ runs cannot exceed $\sqrt{n}$ times the mean, since one run can carry at most the whole sum; a raw fitness with a positive optimum is not so bounded, and the check will flag such a pair falsely, which is one more reason to read the count as approximate. Leaving aside means printed as zero, which is rounding, and summary rows, \nCrcAlImpossiblePairs{} of the re-read census's tested groups, in \nCrcAlImpossiblePairsPapers{} papers and \nCrcAlImpossiblePairsSeparate{} of them in separate-column or separate-row tables, break that bound on one arm; \nCrcAlImpossiblePairsFail{} of them fail Welch's test. Some are the reader pairing a mean with a deviation that is not its own; at least one is the authors' own printed pair, an inconsistency of the paper rather than of the reader, and the two cannot be told apart without a person. Without them the re-read census reads \nCrcAlWithoutImpossibleFail{} \nCrcAlWithoutImpossibleFailCi{} on \nCrcAlWithoutImpossibleTested{} tested groups. The check is not a bound in either direction: it misses every mis-pairing small enough to be possible, and on a raw fitness it flags a pair that is the authors' own; what it does say is that the mis-pairings it catches fail more often than the rest, which is the direction in which the ones it misses push.

\paragraph{Readings on the re-read census} The alternative conventions of the companion audits are rows of Table~3 of the main text; reading the \nCrcAlUnlabelledTested{} tested groups whose bar is unlabelled as standard errors moves the census rate from \nCrcAllFail{} to \nCrcAlUnlabelledAsSe{}. The \nCrcAlCountFromPaperSentences{} tested groups not counted from the table's caption or page take their count from the paper's run sentences. The reader finds a run count for \nCrcTablesWithRunCount{} of the results tables, from the caption in \nCrcNSourceCaption{} cases, the page in \nCrcNSourcePage{} and the run sentences in \nCrcNSourcePaper{}; the modal count is \nCrcRunsRankOneN{} (\nCrcRunsRankOneCount{} tables), \nCrcRunsTestedNThirty{} of the tested groups are at thirty and \nCrcRunsTestedNAtMostFive{} at five or fewer.

\section{The classifier's prompt}
\label{app:prompt}

The paper-kind classifier of Section~4 of the main text received, for each listed paper, the text below with the title and the first three thousand characters of the abstract in place of the placeholders; it saw nothing else of the paper. The labels are those of the released code, in the order given there.

\begin{footnotesize}
\begin{verbatim}
You are classifying a paper that mentions a metaheuristic into exactly one kind.
Labels (answer with the label id only):
- new_optimizer: proposes a new metaheuristic, or a modified / improved / hybrid
  variant of one, and evaluates it against other optimizers
- comparison: compares or benchmarks existing optimizers, or studies their behaviour,
  without proposing a new one as the main contribution
- application: applies one or more metaheuristics to a domain problem (engineering
  design, scheduling, energy, medicine, machine learning tuning ...) and the
  contribution is the application
- survey_theory: a survey, review, tutorial, or a theoretical or methodological paper
  without a new optimizer
- other: anything else, or not about metaheuristics at all

Also say: is the proposed or studied optimizer named after a natural, animal, human or
  physical metaphor (e.g. grey wolf, whale, hippopotamus, political, chemical) rather
  than a mechanism (yes/no/none)? Does the abstract mention benchmark functions or a
  CEC test suite (yes/no)?

Answer as JSON: {"label": "<id>", "confidence": "high|medium|low", "metaphor_named":
  "yes|no|none", "benchmark_suite": "yes|no"}

Title: {title}

Abstract:
{abstract}
\end{verbatim}
\end{footnotesize}

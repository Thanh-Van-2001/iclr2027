ABSTRACT
This paper studies output-feedback security tracking control for
robotic systems under denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, unknown
nonlinearities, and external disturbances. A finite-time state ob-
server based on radial basis function neural networks (RBFNNs)
is developed to reconstruct unmeasured states and approximate
the lumped uncertainty from output measurements. An adaptive
nonlinear filter is then introduced to attenuate disturbance effects
and approximation errors, and an output-feedback security con-
troller is synthesized using observer and filter signals. Lyapunov
analysis shows that, under an average dwell-time condition on DoS
intervals, all closed-loop signals remain bounded, the tracking error
is uniformly ultimately bounded, and the state estimates converge
in finite time. Comparative simulations on a nonlinear unmanned
marine system show consistent improvements in tracking accuracy,
disturbance rejection, and resilience to packet dropouts.
